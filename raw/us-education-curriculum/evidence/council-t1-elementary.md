## 0. Bottom line [Inferred]

T1 is not one band. It is two developmental bands wearing one label — a Grade 2 child (age 7, end of K–2 endpoint) and a Grade 5 child (age 10, end of 3–5 endpoint) need different depth, different vocabulary, and different maths. Every ruling below therefore splits T1 internally into **T1a (Grade 2)** and **T1b (Grades 3–5)** for tagging and sequencing, while keeping one T1 shelf for the child. If Meridian ships T1 as a flat 7–10 pool, the youngest learners will meet Grade 5 content and the oldest will be bored by Grade 2 content — Lane 4 shows this risk is already real: today's `7-10` band holds 56 items with no grade field anywhere in the record type (Lane 4, Registry, [Tested]).

## 1. T1 CALIBRATION RULINGS

### 1.0 The Grade 2 straddle — RULING: separate handling inside T1 is mandatory [Inferred]

- **Ruling:** Keep one T1 tier for families, but tag every T1 item `T1a` (Grade 2-ready) or `T1b` (Grades 3–5) internally, gate T1b items out of a Grade 2 profile, and write Grade 2 cases to the K–2 endpoint only.
- **Anchor:** Lane 1 states the straddle explicitly — NGSS and C3 use K–2 / 3–5 bands, Framework endpoints fall at end of grades 2, 5, 8, 12, so "Meridian T1 (Grades 2–5) therefore straddles the K–2 / 3–5 boundary… T1 content for Grade 2 should use the K–2 endpoint, Grades 3–5 the 3–5 endpoint" (Lane 1, Band-alignment note + §2a, [Inferred] synthesis on [Moderate]/[Low] sources). Lane 1 §6.1 repeats it as a cross-framework synthesis: "Grade 2 needs separate handling inside T1" [Inferred].
- **Anchor:** C3 has the same split — bands are K–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12 (Lane 1, §3a, [Moderate 1/1]).
- **Anchor:** State practice confirms grade-by-grade, not band-flat, delivery at this age: Texas elementary math and science are grade-by-grade §111.4–§111.7 / §112.4–§112.7 (Lane 2, §1a, [Moderate 1/1]); New York science PEs are grade-by-grade PreK–Grade 5 (Lane 2, §2a, [Moderate 1/1]); California science is grade-specific K–5 (Lane 2, §3a, [Moderate 1/1]).
- **What this means in practice [Inferred]:** Grade 2 gets no fractions as numbers, no unlike-denominator work, no multi-digit formal algorithms as a core expectation, no "particles too small to see" language, and social-studies verbs at identify/describe level. Grades 3–5 get the 3–5 endpoints below. A single "ages 7–10" difficulty setting cannot express this.

### 1.1 Math — RULING: arithmetic + measurement + early fractions, ramped by grade; no formal algebra, no symbols-first work [Inferred]

**Belongs at T1, by sub-band:**

| Sub-band | Belongs | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| T1a (Gr 2) | Whole-number addition/subtraction, place value to hundreds, skip-counting, equal groups as preparation for multiplication, measuring with standard units, time and money in everyday contexts | Concrete and pictorial first; numbers in service of a story, not drill sheets |
| T1b (Gr 3–5) | Multiplication/division facts and multi-digit work building across the band, place value/rounding, perimeter and area of rectangles, elapsed time, fractions on the ramp below | Gr 3–4: models and visual reasoning. Gr 5: first formal procedures (see fractions) |

- **Anchor — K–5 foundation:** "The K-5 standards provide students with a solid foundation in whole numbers, addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, fractions and decimals" (Lane 1, §1a, [Moderate 1/1]). T1 domains are Operations & Algebraic Thinking; Number & Operations in Base Ten; Number & Operations — Fractions (from Grade 3); Measurement & Data; Geometry (Lane 1, §1a, [Inferred] synthesis, [Moderate 1/1] for the fractions source).
- **Anchor — formal algebra is NOT T1:** "Formal algebra starts in T2, not T1: Expressions & Equations spans Grades 6–8 and 'mark a shift from arithmetic to algebra'" (Lane 1, §1d, [Low 1/1]). Cut any T1 case that asks a child to manipulate a letter variable as the core move; a missing-number box in a story (e.g. "□ + 7 = 12") is arithmetic reasoning, not algebra, and is acceptable [Inferred].
- **Inventory check:** Meridian's T1 maths is the strongest cell in the pack — 22 items at 7–10 (Lane 4, §(a)–(b), [Tested]) covering skip-counting, equal groups, place value, tables, fair-share division, remainders, measurement, perimeter and rectangle area. That core is correctly placed. Its problem is internal ordering, not topic choice — see the fractions ruling and §2 cuts.

**FRACTIONS RAMP — explicit ruling [Inferred]:**

1. **Grade 2 (T1a): No fraction-as-number instruction.** Sharing fairly ("half for you, half for me") as language in a story is fine; a scored fraction concept is not.
2. **Grade 3: Fraction as a number on the number line; equivalence only in special cases, with visual models.**
3. **Grade 4: Equivalence as a/b = (n×a)/(n×b) via visual models; add/subtract with like denominators only.**
4. **Grade 5: Add/subtract with unlike denominators via equivalent fractions — this is the first and only T1 point where unlike-denominator work belongs.**

- **Anchor:** "Fractions are a multi-year ramp, not a T1 one-shot… Placing formal unlike-denominator work before Grade 5 would be ahead of CCSS" (Lane 1, §1d, [Moderate 1/1], thecorestandards.org-hosted CCSS presentation). The grade-by-grade progression (Gr 3 number line / Gr 4 equivalence + like denominators / Gr 5 unlike denominators) is stated in Lane 1, §1a [Moderate 1/1].
- **Consequence for the inventory [Inferred]:** Lane 4 lists `math-number:fractions:compare-unlike` inside the flat 7–10 band [Tested]. As written, a 7-year-old can be dealt a Grade 5 expectation. Retag it T1b-Grade-5-only. Likewise gate `big-products` and `big-quotients` to Grades 4–5 and keep `fair-parts` / `equivalent` visual, not procedural, at Grades 3–4.

### 1.2 Physics — RULING: qualitative forces, motion and energy transfer only; no formulas, no quantified energy [Inferred]

- **Belongs at T1:** Pushes and pulls and which way things move; balanced vs unbalanced pushes in tug/seesaw terms; magnets attracting/repelling and acting at a distance in observable terms; falling and gravity as "Earth pulls things down"; friction as grip vs slip you can feel and compare; ramps — steeper/faster hits harder, in words; light blocking (shadows) and bouncing (mirrors); sound as vibration you can see/feel; energy moved by moving objects, sound, light and electric currents, in story terms.
- **Depth ceiling:** Words and comparisons ("faster," "stronger push," "steeper") — never F = ma, never KE calculations, never a precise definition of energy.
- **Anchors:** Elementary forces are "pushes/pulls, balanced/unbalanced forces qualitatively (3-PS2-1/2 family)" (Lane 1, §2e, [Low 1/1]); the quantified law HS-PS2-1 (Fnet = ma) is high school, and Lane 1's design rule is "Do not place quantified F = ma problem-solving in T1 or T2 as a core expectation" (Lane 1, §2e, [Moderate 1/1] for HS-PS2-1; design rule [Inferred]). Energy at 3–5 is only "'the faster a given object is moving, the more energy it possesses,' energy moved by moving objects, sound, light, electric currents (4-PS3-1/2), boundary: 'no attempt is made to give a precise or complete definition of energy'" (Lane 1, §2c PS3.A, [Moderate 1/1]). KE proportional to mass and to the square of speed is the end-of-Grade-8 endpoint, not T1 (Lane 1, §2e, [Moderate 1/1]).
- **Inventory check:** Meridian's 12 T1 physics items (Lane 4, §(b), [Tested]) — push which-way/equal-pushes, magnet flip, drop down, friction slide, ramp, lever seesaw, shadow, mirror — sit correctly inside this ceiling. Keep them. Do not import the 10–18 items `physics:ramp:slope-ratio` or `physics:arrive:unit-rate` downward; ratio is a T2 move [Inferred, from Lane 1 §1a T2 ratios anchor, [Low 2/2]].

### 1.3 Chemistry — RULING: YES — at T1 Chemistry is states of matter, observable properties, mixtures and reversible change only [Inferred]

- **Belongs at T1, exhaustively:** (1) Solids and liquids described and sorted by observable properties (colour, hardness, flexibility, absorbency, magnetic or not); (2) melting/freezing and heating/cooling changes, including that some changes reverse and some do not; (3) mixtures — making them, separating them (sieving, filtering, magnets, evaporation in story terms), and dissolving as an observable event at Grades 4–5; (4) conservation in child terms at Grade 5 — weigh it before and after it melts/dissolves, the weight is still there; (5) at Grade 5 only, a guided mix-two-things investigation where children observe whether a new substance seems to appear (fizz, colour change, warmth) — described, never explained by particles.
- **Explicitly excluded at T1:** Atoms, molecules, protons/electrons, the periodic table, chemical symbols/equations, "particles" as a defined explanation, acids/bases, reaction rates. The word "tiny pieces too small to see" may appear at Grade 5 as a phenomenon prompt ("the sugar seems to disappear — where did it go?") but must not be taught as a defined particle model.
- **Anchors:** PS1.A progression — K–2: matter "described/classified by observable properties, solid/liquid depending on temperature (2-PS1-1)"; 3–5: "matter subdivided into particles too small to see, amount (weight) conserved when it changes form (5-PS1-2), properties used to identify materials, with explicit boundary 'mass and weight are not distinguished, and no attempt is made to define the unseen particles' (5-PS1-3)" (Lane 1, §2c, [Moderate 1/1]). Atomic substructure (protons/neutrons/electrons, periodic table by proton number) is HS-PS1-1 (Lane 1, §2e, [Moderate 1/1]). Atoms combining into molecules is the 6–8 step (MS-PS1-1) (Lane 1, §2c, [Moderate 1/1]) — i.e. T2, not T1.
- **Inventory check:** Meridian's 5 T1 chemistry items (Lane 4, §(b), [Tested]) — `states:solid-liquid`, `states:melt-freeze`, `materials:sort`, `materials:magnet`, `pieces:rebuild` — are exactly the right size and scope. This is the best-calibrated science cell in the inventory. Guardrail [Inferred]: `pieces:rebuild` must stay a building/rebuilding-with-materials case; the moment its explanation invokes unseen particles as the taught answer, it breaches the NGSS boundary above.

### 1.4 General Knowledge (Geography / Earth & Space / Interdisciplinary) — RULING: Earth systems and space patterns in observable terms; geography as place, weather and water — not landmark arithmetic [Inferred]

- **Belongs at T1:** Day/night and shadows changing through the day; Moon's changing appearance and seasons/longer-shorter days as observed patterns; weather — measuring and describing it, spotting patterns, preparing for severe weather; water — where it flows, rain, puddles/rivers/seas, the water cycle as a story of movement (no formal systems vocabulary required); rocks, soil and land shapes changing slowly (erosion as "water and wind wear land away"); maps as pictures of a place from above (shared with Social Studies, §1.5); natural resources and caring for a place in concrete terms.
- **Depth:** Observation, pattern-spotting and "what happens next" prediction. No plate tectonics as a mechanism, no geological time scale, no climate-change modelling at T1.
- **Anchors:** NGSS maps ESS1/ESS2/ESS3 to General Knowledge — Earth & Space at "T1: 3–5 ESS (weather, Earth systems, space patterns)" (Lane 1, §2d, [Inferred] mapping). ESS escalation is visible in ESS1.C: by end of Grade 2 "some Earth events are cycles, some quick, some very slow"; by end of Grade 5 "landforms/weathering/erosion infer landscape history" — while the geological time scale is end of Grade 8 and radiometric dating Grade 12 (Lane 1, §2c, [Low 1/1]).
- **State/district anchor:** Elementary Earth topics sit inside integrated science in all three states at T1 (Lane 2, §4 synthesis table, [Inferred] from [Moderate] state sources), and NYC Grade 2 already mixes "Changes to Land Over Time (earth science: land/water shapes, erosion)" with physical and life units in one year (Lane 3, §1(b), [Low 1/1] — secondary mirror of the official NYC scope & sequence).
- **Inventory warning:** Meridian's current T1 General Knowledge is 5 items and none of them is Earth & Space science — they are landmark/arithmetic hybrids (`opera-years-ago`, `eiffel-antennas`, `older-landmark`, `falls-in-km`, `trip-buses`) (Lane 4, §(b), [Tested]). As a T1 GK curriculum this cell is mislabelled maths enrichment, not a geography/Earth offer. See §2 cuts and §4 prompts, which rebuild this cell.

### 1.5 Social Studies — RULING: expanding horizons — self → community → state → nation/hemisphere; verbs are describe/explain; civics and economics stay concrete [Inferred]

- **Belongs at T1, by strand:**
  - *History:* Then-and-now change in the child's own community; stories of people who made a difference; the Grade 4 state-history year as a "My State" slot (see §3); Grade 5 early U.S. / Western Hemisphere story to roughly the 1800s. Continuity ("what stayed the same?") alongside change.
  - *Civics:* Rules and laws in classroom/school/community, why we have them, who helps a community (mayor, firefighters, librarians as roles, not a branches-of-government chart); fairness, voting as a way a group decides, taking part (helping, sharing a view respectfully). Formal structure — branches, levels, constitutions — appears only at the top of T1b as naming, not analysis.
  - *Economics:* Needs vs wants, scarcity as "we can't have everything, so we choose," jobs people do, buying/selling and simple prices, saving for something. No supply/demand graphs, no national economy.
  - *Human Geography:* Maps and globes to locate and describe places; how weather/land/water shape how people live, dress, build and travel; why a town sits by a river; people moving and bringing foods, languages and celebrations.
- **Depth — follow the verb:** "3–5 = describe / explain / identify / compare; 6–8 = assess / analyse…; 9–12 = evaluate / critique" (Lane 1, §3c, [Inferred] from verified indicators). Maps: at 3–5 children "*use* maps of different scales to describe locations (D2.Geo.3.3-5)" — creating maps with geospatial technology is 9–12 (Lane 1, §3c, [Low 1/1]).
- **Anchors:** C3 3–5 civics indicators are at distinguish/explain level — e.g. D2.Civ.1.3-5 distinguish responsibilities/powers of officials, D2.Civ.12.3-5 "explain how rules/laws change society and people change rules/laws" (Lane 1, §3a, [Low 1/1]). Economics at K–2 already grounds scarcity as decision-making (D2.Eco.1.K-2) (Lane 1, §3a, [Low 1/1]) — so concrete choice/scarcity belongs even in T1a.
- **Anchors — sequence:** All three states run an expanding-horizons sequence with a state-history year at Grade 4 — TX Texas History, NY Local History & Local Government, CA "California: A Changing State" (Lane 2, D4, [Moderate 2/3]); Grade 5 is U.S. History (TX), The Western Hemisphere (NY), or U.S. History & Geography to 1850 (CA) (Lane 2, D4, [Moderate 2/3]). District practice matches: NYC Passport runs Gr 2 Rights/Rules/Responsibilities → Communities → Community Geography; Gr 3 World Communities; Gr 4 New York State → Native Americans → Colonial/Revolutionary → Government → Immigration; Gr 5 Western Hemisphere (Lane 3, §1(c), [Moderate 1/1]); CPS Grade 2 is "Families, Neighborhoods, and Communities" through history/geography/economy/government lenses and Grade 5 "Our Nation, Our World" runs to the late 1800s (Lane 3, §3(c), [Moderate 1/1] each).
- **Inventory warning:** Meridian's T1 Social Studies is only 4 items and all four are spatial-maths (`region:area-by-squares`, `route:count-the-steps`, `route:trip-clock`, `place:compass-clue`) (Lane 4, §(b), [Tested]). There is no history, no civics and no economics case at T1 at all. That is the largest content hole at this tier — larger than the Life Science hole in classroom time, because here even the label promises what is absent. §4 rebuilds it.

### 1.6 Journals — RULING: a process layer, not a subject — observation, dating, sorting evidence, and "how do I know?" [Inferred]

- **Belongs at T1:** Looking carefully and drawing/describing what you actually saw; writing the date, place and who/what on a note or photo; sorting notes/photos into "what happened first/next/last" and "what goes together"; asking a person a question and writing down their words; keeping a simple diary over days/weeks (weather, a plant, the Moon) and spotting the pattern; ending a note with "I think ___ because I saw ___."
- **Depth:** Short, concrete, scaffolded. Text complexity at the 2–3 band for Grade 2–3 and the 4–5 band for Grades 4–5. No research essay, no citation apparatus, no argument evaluation at T1.
- **Anchors:** "CCSS does not create a 'journal' subject. Journals maps to RI (reading informational/technical text) + Writing W.3.8-type expectations (recall/gather information, take brief notes, sort evidence)" and should be "calibrated as applied informational reading + note-taking / evidence-sorting, escalating by text-complexity band (2–3, 4–5, 6–8…)" (Lane 1, §1b, [Inferred] on [Moderate 1/1] CCSS source; RI.3.3 explicitly covers describing relationships between scientific ideas/steps using sequence and cause/effect language, and RI.3.10 sets the grades 2–3 complexity band). Lane 2 agrees from the state side: "Journals is a Meridian-original category and should be designed as a cross-cutting practice layer, not a standards-aligned subject" (Lane 2, §4, [Inferred]); no TX/NY/CA standard names a Journals subject (Lane 2, §6, gap 2).
- **Inventory check:** Journals has 8 T1 items — the joint-largest science-adjacent cell — but they are landmark/technology story cases (`lincoln-memorial`, `first-phone`, `eniac`, `greenwich-zero`, `liverpool-manchester`, `hearts-content-cable`, `pont-du-gard`, `akashi-span`) with, uniquely, no clue scenes — "each case shows the clip's caption text as its lead" (Lane 4, §(b)–(c), [Tested]). As stories they are enjoyable enrichment; as a Journals curriculum they teach almost none of the process skills above. Keep at most a minority as story content; rebuild the majority as do-it-yourself field-note cases (§4).

## 2. BLOAT CUTS — what to cut from a concise T1 Meridian curriculum, and why [All Inferred]

A concise curriculum is a promise a busy teacher and a 20-minute attention span can keep. Cut for miscalibration, duplication, or abstraction a 7–10-year-old cannot use — not for lack of charm.

1. **CUT from T1 (defer to T2/T3): all ratio, rate and formal-proportion cases wearing T1 clothes.** Inventory items such as `falls-in-km` (unit conversion as the core move) and any slope-ratio/unit-rate reasoning belong where Lane 1 places ratios — Grade 6 (Lane 1, §1a, [Low 2/2]). A Grade 4 child comparing "which waterfall is taller" in metres with a picture is measurement; converting km as the puzzle is T2.
2. **CUT: landmark-arithmetic trivia as General Knowledge.** `opera-years-ago`, `older-landmark`, `eiffel-antennas`, `trip-buses` (Lane 4, [Tested]) test date subtraction and bus division, not geography or Earth science. They duplicate Math while leaving ESS1–ESS3 (Lane 1, §2b, [Moderate 1/1]) untaught at T1. Keep one landmark story if children love it — in Journals, as a story — and give the GK slots to weather, water, Moon and land (§4).
3. **CUT: the Social Studies / Math double-count.** `region:area-by-squares` is Math's `rect-area` in a map costume, and `route:trip-clock` is Math's `elapsed-time` (all Lane 4, §(b), [Tested]). One case, one home: area and elapsed time live in Math; Social Studies slots go to the missing history/civics/economics (§1.5). Lane 1 flags the underlying geography double-home (GK vs Social Studies) as a synthesis decision (Lane 1, §3d, [Inferred]) — my ruling: physical/spatial basics (maps-as-pictures, land/water, weather) → General Knowledge; people, rules, past and choices → Social Studies [Inferred].
4. **CUT from T1a/T1b-early: unlike-denominator fraction comparison and big-number procedure cases as open-pool items.** Not deleted from T1 — gated to Grade 5 / Grades 4–5 per §1.1. In a flat pool they are bloat for 80% of the tier's age span (Lane 1, §1d, [Moderate 1/1]).
5. **CUT: abstract technology-history at T1 Journals.** `technology:eniac` and `hearts-content-cable` (transatlantic cable) (Lane 4, [Tested]) ask a 7–10-year-old to care about systems they cannot see, touch or retell. The kid test in Meridian's own philosophy — one memorable hook a child can retell — fails here. Replace with field notes about technology in the child's hand (a phone camera, a barcode, a magnet on a fridge) or defer to T2, where Lane 4 already holds the `mobile-phone` and `:stretch` variants.
6. **CUT: any T1 science explanation that names a precise definition of energy, a particle model, or a chemical mechanism.** This is bloat of the worst kind — words without a phenomenon attached. NGSS bars it explicitly at this band for both energy (Lane 1, §2c, [Moderate 1/1]) and particles (Lane 1, §2c, [Moderate 1/1]). If the proved explanation cannot be shown with hands, water, a ramp or a shadow, it does not belong at T1.
7. **CUT: state-specific history content hard-coded for all learners.** A full Texas History or California History case set is core curriculum in Austin or Sacramento and dead weight in Chicago. Lane 2's design consequence is a state-agnostic "My State" slot at Grade 4 (Lane 2, D4, [Inferred] on [Moderate 2/3]). Meridian should ship the slot, the inquiry shape and one worked example — not 50 state curricula [Inferred].
8. **NOT a cut — a refusal to add:** Do not add a T1 "test prep / civics test" strand. A standalone civics-test graduation requirement was "not verified as a current requirement" in TX/NY/CA — "Data not available — do not design a civics-test requirement into Meridian on this evidence" (Lane 2, D8). Elementary civics is participation, not a memorised question bank [Inferred].

## 3. DIVERGENCES — district practice (Lane 3) vs standards documents (Lanes 1–2) at T1, and which Meridian should follow [Inferred rulings on cited evidence]

**Divergence 1 — Science is labelled by discipline in the standards, but taught mixed in every district classroom.**
Lanes 1–2 organise elementary science by strand (NGSS PS/LS/ESS; NY grade-by-grade PEs across Physical/Life/Earth & Space, Lane 2, §2a, [Moderate 1/1]). Lane 3 shows children never meet it that way: NYC Grade 2 runs matter/water, land change and plant/animal units inside one year (Lane 3, §1(b), [Low 1/1]); NYC middle-school SCALE is explicitly integrated and elementary follows the same PK–8 scope document (Lane 3, §1(b), [Moderate 1/1]); CPS K–5 science is phenomenon-based with life, physical and earth all present (Lane 3, §3(b), [Moderate 2/2]); Miami-Dade organises K–8 science by four Bodies of Knowledge — Nature of Science, Earth & Space, Life, Physical — "across K-5 and 6-8," integrated every year (Lane 3, §4(b), [Low 1/1]).
**Follow: the districts, for delivery; the standards, for coverage.** Meridian T1 science cases should be shelved for the child as mixed "science stories" in term-sized bundles (a water bundle, a living-things bundle), while the teacher-facing map tracks PS/ESS/LS coverage against NGSS so no strand silently vanishes. Shelving T1 cases under the adult labels "Physics" and "Chemistry" misdescribes every documented elementary classroom [Inferred].

**Divergence 2 — Social Studies content order exists only in districts/states, not in C3.**
C3 "does not tell Meridian which historical events, countries, or economic case studies to teach" and "intentionally preserves… selection of curricular content… as a decision best made by each state" (Lane 1, §3a, [Low 1/1]; implication [Inferred]). The actual T1 order — expanding horizons, Grade 4 state year, Grade 5 hemisphere/U.S. — comes from Lane 2 state frameworks (D4, [Moderate 2/3]) and Lane 3 district curricula (NYC Passport, [Moderate 1/1]; CPS Grade 2/5 maps, [Moderate 1/1]).
**Follow: districts/states for sequence, C3 for skill verbs.** Build T1 Social Studies in the Passport/CPS order (community → world communities → my state → hemisphere/early nation) and calibrate every case's task verb to C3 3–5 (describe/explain/compare). Using C3 alone would produce skills with nothing to practise on [Inferred].

**Divergence 3 — Maths sequence is national, but maths curriculum is local and non-uniform.**
CCSS/NY Next Generation/CA CCSS all give grade-by-grade T1 maths (Lane 1, §1a; Lane 2, §§1a/2a/3a). Lane 3 shows no single enacted elementary sequence: NYC elementary maths is district-chosen (District 5 i-Ready, Districts 11/12 Illustrative Mathematics, District 25 Amplify Desmos) with "no single citywide elementary math sequence" (Lane 3, §1(a), [Moderate 1/1], inference flagged there); LAUSD uses per-grade Eureka Math maps (Lane 3, §2(a), [Moderate 1/1]); CPS uses its own Skyline Math (Lane 3, §3(a), [High 2/2] for Skyline's existence).
**Follow: the standards for the learning order, no district for the brand.** Meridian's T1 maths order should track the CCSS ramp (§1.1) — which all three district programmes also implement — and should not clone Eureka/Skyline/Illustrative unit order, because there is no national classroom that follows any one of them [Inferred].

**Divergence 4 — Civics timing: standards expect early civics concepts; districts split between early-lite and late-heavy.**
C3 expects real civics at 3–5 (distinguish officials' responsibilities, explain how people change rules/laws — Lane 1, §3a, [Low 1/1]). District practice spreads it: NYC teaches Rights, Rules & Responsibilities in Grade 2 and runs a Civics for All strand (Lane 3, §1(c), [Moderate 1/1]); Miami-Dade flags Grade 7 Civics EOC benchmarks "inside guides from grade 3 up" while the full civics year sits in Grade 7 (Lane 3, §4(c), [Moderate 1/1]); CPS places Civics at Grades 8 & 11 in its banding (Lane 3, §3(c), [Moderate 2/2]).
**Follow: NYC/C3 at T1 — civics-lite, early, lived.** Classroom rules, community helpers, a class vote and "how could we change this rule fairly?" fully satisfy C3 3–5 and match what 7–10-year-olds can do. Do not import Miami's Grade 7 EOC shadow or CPS's Grade 8 placement downward into T1 task design [Inferred].

**Divergence 5 — Houston and Miami remind us district evidence itself is partial at T1.**
HISD publishes no public grade-by-grade scope & sequence (Lane 3, §5, [Moderate 2/2] for the access situation); Miami-Dade pacing guides are portal-gated and publicly reachable only as mirrors (Lane 3, §4, [Low 1/1] for most contents). Where Lane 3 is silent, Meridian should not pretend a national district consensus exists.
**Follow: the three well-documented districts (NYC, LAUSD, CPS) + Lanes 1–2; mark HISD/Miami T1 topic order "Data not available"** rather than extrapolating it [Inferred].

**Non-divergence worth naming [Inferred]:** On integrated elementary science and on the Grade 4 state-history year, standards, states and districts all agree (Lane 2, §4: "Never a standalone subject; inside integrated elementary science everywhere"; D4, [Moderate 2/3]). Meridian can build those two structures without choosing a side.

## 4. CASE-FILE PROMPTS — T1 COMPLETE
### 24 prompts: 6 categories × 4. Format per Meridian's picker: hook question (the "Why Do We Learn This?" case) + real-world situation + concept taught.
### Every prompt is **[Inferred]** — original work for this memo. All are conceptual, everyday-world, no formal formulas, calibrated T1b (Grades 3–5) unless marked T1a-safe. None duplicates a Lane 4 T1 topic; near-neighbours are flagged.

#### Math (extends Lane 4 T1 maths beyond number/measurement into data, geometry and money — domains present in Lane 1 §1a but absent from the inventory)

1. **[Inferred] "Why do we draw picture graphs instead of just saying the numbers?"** — Situation: the class votes on a pet for the class corner and needs everyone to see the winner at a glance. Concept: data representation — one picture stands for a count; reading and comparing a pictograph/bar graph. *(New: no data/graph item exists in Lane 4 T1 maths.)*

## 5. AGREEMENTS & DISSENTS

### 5.1 What I expect the full council to agree on [Inferred]

1. **The Grade 2 straddle is real and needs internal T1a/T1b handling** — it is stated in Lane 1's own synthesis (§6.1) and follows mechanically from the K–2/3–5 bands in both NGSS and C3. I expect no seat to defend a flat 7–10 pool.
2. **Quantification is the STEM difficulty dial** — T1 qualitative, T2 relational, T3 formal (Lane 1, §6.2). No F = ma, no defined particles, no precise energy definition, no mole at T1. The NGSS boundaries are explicit text, not taste.
3. **The fractions ramp (Gr 3 → 4 → 5)** and the deferral of formal algebra to T2 (Lane 1, §1d).
4. **Journals is a process/practice layer, not a content subject** — agreed independently by Lane 1 (§1b, §6.4) and Lane 2 (§4) from different evidence.
5. **Grade 4 = a state-agnostic "My State" slot;** the structure is national, the content is local (Lane 2, D4).
6. **Meridian's current T1 Social Studies and General Knowledge cells do not deliver what their labels promise** — 4 spatial-maths items and 5 landmark-arithmetic items respectively (Lane 4, [Tested]). Any seat reading Lane 4 first-hand will land here.

### 5.2 Where I dissent — stated plainly [Inferred]

**DISSENT 1 — Life Science housing: I dissent from quietly housing Life Science inside General Knowledge.**
The pack's apparent direction is Lane 1 §2d/§2f option (b): LS1–LS4 mapped to "General Knowledge — Interdisciplinary" for want of a home, with Lane 1 explicitly taking no position (§2f). From the elementary classroom, that housing will fail, for three evidence-based reasons:

- Life Science is not a garnish at this tier — it is roughly a third of enacted elementary science. NYC Grade 2's three documented units are one physical, one earth, one life (Lane 3, §1(b), [Low 1/1]); Miami-Dade names Life Science as one of four Bodies of Knowledge present every year K–8 (Lane 3, §4(b), [Low 1/1]); CPS's discipline mix across K–8 is life + physical + earth (Lane 3, §3(b), [Moderate 2/2]); NGSS gives LS four full DCIs (LS1–LS4) in every band (Lane 1, §2b, [Moderate 1/1]).
- General Knowledge at T1 is already the weakest, most contested cell — 5 items, none of them science (Lane 4, [Tested]) — and it already carries Geography *and* Earth & Space *and* "Interdisciplinary." Adding a whole hidden discipline to the smallest shelf guarantees it is crowded out by the geography content the label actually advertises.
- Children aged 7–10 learn science most readily from living things — plants growing, animals' homes, their own bodies and senses, life cycles they can watch in a term. An elementary curriculum in which Life Science is invisible will read to teachers as a curriculum with the most teachable third missing, and they will supplement it ad hoc, breaking Meridian's coherence.

**My position [Inferred]:** First choice — a seventh category, Life Science / Living World, at least at T1–T2 where it carries equal classroom weight. If the 6-category structure is immovable, then General Knowledge must be formally split on the teacher-facing map into named strands (Earth & Space / Living World / Places) with a protected minimum share for Living World at T1 — I recommend no less than one-third of T1 GK cases — and the child-facing label must show living things in its one-liner. What I will not sign is LS absorbed invisibly with no strand name and no protected share. (Per the assignment, I have not written Life Science prompts into §4's General Knowledge set, because that set was specified as Geography/Earth & Space — but the four prompts a T1 Living World strand needs first are: bean-plant growth diary; why animals' homes match their place; life cycle of a butterfly/frog as a story of stages; senses and body systems in "how do I know it's sweet/hot/loud?" form. All [Inferred], all squarely inside LS1/LS2 3–5 endpoints as characterised in Lane 1, §2b/§2d.)

**DISSENT 2 — "Physics" and "Chemistry" as child-facing T1 labels.**
The 6-category design keeps adult discipline names at every tier. No documented elementary system teaches a subject called Physics or Chemistry at Grades 2–5 — Texas, New York and California all teach integrated "Science" (Lane 2, §1a/§2a/§3a, [Moderate 1/1] each; Lane 2, §4: "Never a standalone subject"). I expect other seats (especially T3) to defend the labels for continuity. My dissent is narrow [Inferred]: keep Physics/Chemistry as teacher-facing strand tags at T1 for coverage tracking, but let the child-facing T1 shelf say "Science" bundles (Forces & Motion stories under Physics content is fine internally). A 7-year-old choosing "Chemistry" is choosing a word she cannot yet mean.

**DISSENT 3 — Against importing district pacing as curriculum order.**
Where Lane 3 is strongest (NYC Passport, CPS Skyline) there will be pressure to adopt a documented sequence wholesale. I dissent in part [Inferred]: adopt the *shape* (expanding horizons; mixed science bundles) but not any district's unit order or content picks — §3, Divergence 3 shows even maths has no shared enacted order, and Passport's New York content (NYC Over Time, NYS geography) is exactly the state-specific material §2.7 cuts. Meridian is a national product; districts are evidence about children, not a template.

**Data not available (honest gaps in my own seat's evidence):** Grade-by-grade topic detail inside TX Grades 2–3 social studies and inside NY/CA elementary science beyond the cited sequences (Lane 2, §6, gap 8); HISD and Miami-Dade T1 topic order from public official documents (Lane 3, §§4–5); any T1-specific evidence on how children respond to Meridian's existing case format — Lane 4 is a code inventory, not classroom observation. My calibration rulings rest on standards + enacted-curriculum documents, not on playtesting with 7–10-year-olds, and should be checked against the owner-config playthrough before ship [Inferred].

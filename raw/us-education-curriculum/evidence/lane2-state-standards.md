# Lane 2 — State Standards: Texas, New York, California

**Phase 1 research — Meridian curriculum design | Date: 2026-11-24**
**Scope:** How TX, NY, and CA structure Math, Science (Physics / Chemistry / Earth & Space), and Social Studies (History / Civics / Economics / Human Geography) across Meridian's tiers, and where states diverge from each other and from a single national sequence.
**Research only. No code, no repo changes.**

## How to read this file

- **Meridian tiers used throughout:** T1 Upper Elementary ages 7–10 (Grades 2–5); T2 Middle School ages 11–14 (Grades 6–8); T3 High School ages 14–18 (Grades 9–12); T4 College & Adult 18+.
- **Meridian categories:** Math; Physics; Chemistry; General Knowledge (Geography / Earth & Space / Interdisciplinary); Social Studies (History / Civics / Economics / Human Geography); Journals (applied field notes).
- **Confidence tags:** `[High x/y]` = 3+ agreeing primary sources; `[Moderate x/y]` = 2 agreeing sources or 1 primary source; `[Low x/y]` = 1 secondary source or conflicting sources. x = agreeing sources, y = sources consulted for that claim.
- **Primary source** = official state source (tea.texas.gov / sboe.texas.gov, nysed.gov / regents.nysed.gov, cde.ca.gov). Republished copies of the same state document count as **one** source.
- **[Inferred]** = analyst mapping/interpretation, not stated in the source.
- **"Data not available"** = not verified from an official source in this pass. Nothing is invented to fill a gap — no TEKS codes, course names, or requirements were fabricated.

## 2. NEW YORK — Next Generation Learning Standards + P-12 Science Learning Standards + K-12 Social Studies Framework

New York runs **three different architectures**: (i) Next Generation Learning Standards for Mathematics (2017, fully implemented Sept 2020); (ii) NYS P-12 Science Learning Standards (NY's NGSS-derived science standards); (iii) NYS K-12 Social Studies Framework (approved April 2014, revised 2017 for grades 9–12), built on 5 learning standards: (1) History of the U.S. and New York, (2) World History, (3) Geography, (4) Economics, (5) Civics, Citizenship, and Government. ## 5. DIVERGENCES — design around these

### State vs state

**D1. Earth & Space Science lives in a different tier and a different container in each state. [High]**
- TX: **Earth Systems Science is a standalone named HS course** (§112.49, Adopted 2021), alongside Astronomy — Earth science is a T3 subject a student elects. Source: https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-112 [Moderate 1/1]
- NY: Earth & Space content is in the banded 6–8 and 9–12 PEs **and** Earth and Space Sciences is a Regents examination/course at HS. Source: https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/state-assessment/educator-guide-earth-space-science-2024.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- CA: In the preferred HS model Earth & Space Science is **deliberately not a standalone course** — it is integrated into all three courses (Living Earth / Chemistry in the Earth System / Physics of the Universe); standalone ESS appears only in the alternative 4-course model. Sources: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/sc/cf/documents/scifwchapter7.pdf?ref=stanislav-kondrashov.ghost.io ; https://www.cde.ca.gov:443/ci/sc/cf/documents/scifwforeword.pdf [High 2/2]
- **Design consequence [Inferred]:** Meridian's "Earth & Space" strand inside General Knowledge cannot be placed in one tier. It needs a T2 foundation + a T3 treatment that works both as a standalone course (TX/NY pattern) and as a thread woven through Biology/Chemistry/Physics (CA pattern).

**D2. Physics & Chemistry: standalone vs integrated is a state-level fork at T3 — and TX offers both at once.**
- TX names **Chemistry, Physics, AND Integrated Physics and Chemistry (IPC)** as separate HS courses; IPC can satisfy a science graduation credit. Sources: https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-112 ; https://tea.texas.gov/curriculum-and-instruction/curriculum-standards/teks-review/science-teks-review [High 2/2]
- CA's preferred model has **no standalone Physics or Chemistry course at all** — both are fused with Earth science. Source: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/sc/cf/documents/scifwchapter7.pdf?ref=stanislav-kondrashov.ghost.io [Moderate 1/1]
- NY standards name **neither** — Physics/Chemistry exist at course/Regents level, not in the banded standards document. Source: https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/curriculum-instruction/p-12-science-learning-standards.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- **Design consequence [Inferred]:** Meridian's decision to keep Physics and Chemistry as separate top-level categories matches TX/NY course reality but **not** CA's preferred classroom reality. A CA-facing learner meets "Chemistry in the Earth System," not "Chemistry." Meridian needs cross-walk labels (category = Chemistry; CA course alias = Chemistry in the Earth System) and an IPC-style combined on-ramp at early T3, which only TX models explicitly.

**D3. Personal finance: three different legal shapes, all recent.**
- TX: PFL is **fused with economics** — one 0.5-credit course, 2/3 PFL + 1/3 economics, interchangeable with pure Economics for graduation (2022). Sources: https://tea.texas.gov/academics/subject-areas/social-studies/personal-financial-literacy-and-economics-faq.pdf ; https://sboe.texas.gov/state-board-of-education/sboe-2022/sboe-2022-june/june-14-2022-committee-of-the-full-board-item-5 [High 2/2]
- CA: PFL becomes a **separate standalone one-semester course that may not be combined with any other course**, for pupils graduating 2030–31, and completing it can exempt the economics semester. Source: https://www.cde.ca.gov/Ci/gs/hs/hsgrmin.asp [Moderate 1/1]
- NY: Economics at Gr 12 is titled **"Economics, the Enterprise System, and Finance"** (0.5 unit, required within the 4-unit social studies requirement) — finance is inside the economics course title, with no separate standalone PFL course documented in the Framework/FAQ sources searched. Sources: http://www.nysed.gov/common/nysed/files/programs/curriculum-instruction/framework-9-12-with-2017-updates.pdf ; https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/social-studies-faq [High 2/2]
- **Design consequence [Inferred]:** A single Meridian "Economics" module will misalign somewhere. Economics and Personal Finance should be **separable units** that can be presented fused (TX), standalone (CA future cohorts), or as economics-with-finance (NY).

**D4. Grade 4 is state history everywhere — but it is a different state, and Grade 5 / middle-school history sequences do not line up.**
- Gr 4: TX = Texas History; NY = Local History and Local Government (NY State/local); CA = California: A Changing State. Sources: https://tpwd.texas.gov/state-parks/lyndon-b-johnson/field-trips/guided-tours ; https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/ss-framework-k-12-intro.pdf ; https://www.cde.ca.gov/CI/hs/cf/hssframework.asp [Moderate 2/3 — NY & CA primary; TX topic label rests on a TX state-agency (TPWD) source, not TEA]
- Gr 5: TX = U.S. History; NY = The Western Hemisphere (not U.S. history alone); CA = U.S. History and Geography: Making a New Nation (to 1850). Same sources [Moderate 2/3]
- TX then **repeats state history for a full second year in Gr 7** — no equivalent second state-history year exists in the NY or CA sequences searched (NY folds NY history into the Gr 7–8 U.S. sequence; CA has no Gr 7 state-history year). Sources: https://tpwd.texas.gov/state-parks/lyndon-b-johnson/field-trips/guided-tours ; https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/ss-framework-k-12-intro.pdf ; https://www.cde.ca.gov/CI/hs/cf/hssframework.asp [Moderate 2/3]
- **Design consequence [Inferred]:** Meridian T1 Social Studies needs a **state-agnostic "My State" slot at Grade 4** (and a second one at TX Grade 7) rather than hard-coding any one state's history — the structure is national, the content is irreducibly local.

**D5. High-school social studies: convergent core, divergent world-history and Grade 9 treatment.**
- All three states require at HS: U.S. History (1 yr), Government/Civics (1 semester), Economics (1 semester). Sources: https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/social-studies-faq ; https://www.cde.ca.gov/Ci/gs/hs/hsgrmin.asp ; https://sboe.texas.gov/sites/default/files/COFB_111318_item1_attach1_Ch113_Ch118_TEKS_Social_Studies.pdf [High 3/3]
- World history: NY requires **two full years** (Global History and Geography I + II, Gr 9–10). CA requires one year (Gr 10 Modern World) and makes **Gr 9 an elective year**. TX requires **one credit of World Geography OR World History** (student/district choice) within the 3-credit requirement — world history is not unconditionally required. Same sources [High 3/3]
- **Design consequence [Inferred]:** Meridian T3 Social Studies can safely build one shared U.S. History + Civics + Economics spine, but World History/Geography must be modular: a 2-year NY-depth track, a 1-year CA track, and a TX either/or fork.

**D6. Middle-school science architecture: grade-by-grade vs banded vs integrated-model.**
- TX: grade-by-grade Gr 6, 7, 8 science (§112.26–112.28). Source: https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-112 [Moderate 1/1]
- NY: **banded 6–8** Performance Expectations — the state does not assign science topics to individual middle-school grades. Source: https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/curriculum-instruction/p-12-science-learning-standards.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- CA: grade-span 6–8 standards with a state-preferred **integrated** arrangement across grades. Source: https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngSSstandards.asp [Moderate 1/1]
- **Design consequence [Inferred]:** Meridian T2 science content should be tagged by **topic + readiness band**, not locked to a single grade, or it will fit TX classrooms and misfit NY/CA classrooms (and vice versa).

**D7. Mathematics at T3: CA alone documents an integrated pathway as co-equal.**
- TX & NY standards documents organise HS math as Algebra I / Geometry / Algebra II. CA's framework documents **both** that sequence **and** Mathematics I/II/III as valid pathways to Precalculus/Calculus. Sources: https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-111 ; https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/curriculum-instruction/nys-next-generation-mathematics-p-12-standards.pdf?v=2025030108 ; https://www.cde.ca.gov/CI/ma/cf/mathfwfaqs.asp [High 3/3]
- **Design consequence [Inferred]:** Meridian T3 Math topics should be organised by **concept strand** (functions, geometry, statistics) with two published orderings, not by a single course sequence.

**D8. Civics: a required course everywhere; a separate civics *test* verified nowhere in this pass.**
- All 3 states require a government/civics course or half-unit at T3 (TX U.S. Government 0.5 credit; NY Participation in Government 0.5 unit; CA American government and civics 1 semester). Sources in D5 [High 3/3].
- A standalone **graduation civics test** (e.g., a USCIS-naturalisation-style test) was **not verified as a current requirement** from an official TEA/NYSED/CDE source in this pass — Texas bills proposing one (e.g., HB 1776, 85th Leg.) appear in the legislative record as committee/bill text, which does not by itself prove a current enacted requirement. **Status: Data not available — do not design a civics-test requirement into Meridian on this evidence.**

### State vs national framework [Inferred, bounded]

- NY science and CA science are both **NGSS-derived** (NY: P-12 Science Learning Standards built on the NRC Framework; CA: NGSS adopted as CA NGSS in 2013), yet they diverge from each other in arrangement (NY banded topics vs CA integrated course models) — adoption of the same national framework does **not** produce the same course structure. Sources: https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/curriculum-instruction/p-12-science-learning-standards.pdf ; https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngSSstandards.asp [High 2/2]
- TX TEKS are a **state-authored** system in TAC, not an adoption of Common Core / NGSS documents — TX math and science keep grade-by-grade elementary/middle structures and named HS courses where NY/CA use banding/integration at T2–T3. Sources: https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-111 ; https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/texas-administrative-code/19-tac-chapter-112 [Moderate 1/2 — structural comparison is [Inferred] from the cited TAC organisation]
- CA math **is** Common Core-based (adopted 2010) and NY math is a **state revision** of Common Core (Next Generation, 2017) — so even the two "Common Core" states no longer share an identical math document. Sources: https://www.cde.ca.gov/BE/ST/SS/ ; https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/curriculum-instruction/nys-next-generation-mathematics-p-12-standards.pdf?v=2025030108 [High 2/2]

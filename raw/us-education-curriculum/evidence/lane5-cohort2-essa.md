# Lane 5 — Cohort 2: ESSA-Driven District Sampling + Curriculum Extraction

**Purpose:** Second district cohort for the Meridian curriculum design, sampled for MEASURED performance (high proficiency AND documented growth) strictly from official ESSA accountability sources — to balance Cohort 1 (NYCPS, LAUSD, Chicago, Miami-Dade, Houston ISD), which was big-urban only.
**Meridian tiers referenced:** T1 = Gr 2–5 · T2 = Gr 6–8 · T3 = Gr 9–12 · T4 = 18+.
**Confidence tags:** [High x/y] = 3+ agreeing primary sources · [Moderate x/y] = 2 agreeing, or 1 primary · [Low x/y] = 1 secondary / conflicting. Interpretations are labelled [Inferred].
**Honesty rule applied:** any figure not verified on the official portal (or an official district/state document reproducing that portal) is written as "Data not available" — never estimated.

### Method notes (Part 1)
- **Selection order followed:** state ESSA portals/dashboards first (FDOE School Grades; ADE AZ School Report Cards / A–F public file; NDE Nebraska Education Profile; ISBE Illinois Report Card; TEA Accountability / TXschools.gov; CDE California School Dashboard), NCES for identity/enrollment context only (e.g., St. Johns ≈ 52,322 students, 2024–25; Chandler USD ≈ 41,421 students, 2024–25 — both as carried in the FDOE/ADE-derived district tables above) [Moderate].
- **Family honesty notes:** (i) Arizona and Illinois both assign A–F / summative designations at **school** level; a district "grade" in AZ is an LEA aggregation of the same public file, and in IL no district designation exists — both are recorded as such rather than forced into a district-grade mould. (ii) California does not use a 100-point index at all; Texas does (0–100 scaled scores → A–F). The brief's "index" label fits Texas only.
- **Growth is co-primary:** every district above has a documented growth/change indicator in its own state's terms (FL Learning Gains component + proficiency change; AZ SGP-weighted A–F model; NE Individual Score Growth; IL growth percentile; TX Academic Growth scaled score; CA Dashboard Change). Where the exact district growth *value* could not be retrieved in text form from the portal, the cell says so explicitly instead of substituting an estimate.

## SELECTION RATIONALE (per district)

**1. St. Johns County SD, FL — performance + growth.** Florida's top FAST district in spring 2025: ELA 75.4% and Math 78.3% at Level 3+ were the highest / second-highest district rates in the state (statewide: 56.7% ELA, 58.9% Math), and FDOE named it an Academically High-Performing District for 2025–26, a designation that requires an "A" district grade in two consecutive years and no "F" school — i.e., sustained, not one-year, performance [Moderate 2/3]. Growth: the Florida district grade itself scores Learning Gains in ELA and Math (including gains among the lowest-performing 25%), so an "A" cannot be earned on status alone; the district's ELA proficiency also rose ≈3.4 points year-over-year (calculation in table) [Inferred from the grade formula + published rates]. It replaces Miami-Dade (Cohort 1) with a mid-size (~52k), non-mega-urban Florida system. Portal: https://www.fldoe.org/accountability/accountability-reporting/school-grades/index.stml

**2. Chandler USD #80, AZ — performance + growth.** Chandler earned an "A" as an LEA in 2024–25 with 70.45% of its schools A-rated and 95% A-or-B — the district's highest A/B share under the current model — while posting ELA 58% and Math 53% proficiency against state averages of 39% and 32% (gaps of +19 and +21 points; calculation: district rate − state rate, ADE 2024–25 AASA file, n = Gr 3–8 tested) [Moderate 1/2]. Growth is structural in Arizona's model — Student Growth Percentiles carry 50 of 100 points in the K–8 A–F model — and Chandler's Math proficiency rose 2 points year-over-year with five additional schools moving to A [Moderate 1/2]. It gives the sample a large suburban unified district (~41k students) outside the Cohort-1 coastal-urban set. Portal: https://azreportcards.azed.gov/schools/detail/5964

**3. Arlington Public Schools, NE — performance + growth.** Arlington's 2024–25 NEP snapshot shows a Final AQuESTT classification of **Excellent** (Nebraska's top descriptive tier) with NSCAS proficiency well above state rates on all three subjects — ELA 70% vs 59% state, Math 73% vs 58%, Science 90% vs 80% (gaps +11, +15, +10 points; calculation: district − state, NEP 2024–25) — and, critically for this sample, a published **Individual Score Growth of 55%**, so its tier is not a status-only artefact [Moderate 1/2]. It is also the sample's deliberate small-district counterweight (a Class III PK–12 district serving Arlington/Kennard/Elk City, Nebraska) to Cohort 1's mega-districts. Portal: https://nep.education.ne.gov/

**4. Triad CUSD #2, IL — performance + growth.** Illinois' descriptive system designates schools, not districts; Triad qualifies at school level with two Exemplary schools (its high school in the top 1.7% statewide) and the rest Commendable in the 2024 Report Card, while beating the state on every tested subject — ELA +12.0, Math +14.6, Science +21.1 points vs state rates (calculation: district − state, ISBE 2024) [Moderate 1/2]. Its growth evidence is explicit: the elementary/middle Math growth percentile rose from 51 (2023) to 55.5 (2024) — above the state median of 50 by construction of the percentile — showing a high-growth trajectory on top of above-state proficiency [Moderate 1/2]. Portal: https://www.illinoisreportcard.com/

**5. Eanes ISD, TX — performance + growth.** Eanes is the sample's cleanest index case: TEA's 2025 district summary gives an overall **scaled score of 94 (A)**, with Student Achievement 94 (A) and Closing the Gaps 94 (A), and district STAAR "Meets Grade Level or Above" rates of ≈88% ELA / 79% Math / 81% Science against state Meets rates near 54% / 45% / 47% [Moderate 1/2 for subject rates; High 2/3 for the scaled scores]. Growth is separately scored, not assumed: School Progress Part A (Academic Growth) earned a scaled **82 (B)** — lower than its status scores, which is exactly the kind of status-vs-growth separation this cohort was designed to surface [Inferred]. It replaces Houston ISD (Cohort 1) with a small, high-performing Austin-area district. Portal: TXschools.gov via https://tea.texas.gov/school-and-district-leaders/accountability/academic-accountability/performance-reporting/2025-accountability-rating-system

**6. Irvine USD, CA — performance + growth (with an honest caveat).** Irvine posts California's elite large-district profile: CAASPP proficiency of 74% ELA / 70% Math / 63.09% Science in 2024–25 (state: 48% / 37% / 32.52%) and Dashboard Distance From Standard of +58.5 points in ELA and +49 in Math, with Math at the **Blue (highest)** colour level [Moderate 1/2]. The caveat, recorded rather than smoothed over: California has no growth percentile, and Irvine's Dashboard **Change** was "Maintained" in Math and "Declined" in ELA — i.e., very high status, flat-to-negative change, consistent with a ceiling effect at +50-plus DFS [Inferred]. Irvine is retained because (a) its Change indicator *is* documented (the brief's disqualifier is *undocumented* growth), and (b) it is the clearest case in the cohort for teaching the design team that high proficiency ≠ high growth. Portal: https://www.caschooldashboard.org/reports/30736500000000/2026

## CLOSING NOTE — What Cohort 2 confirms or contradicts about Cohort 1's patterns

*(Only where the Cohort 2 documents retrieved actually speak to the pattern.)*

1. **Integrated middle-school science — CONFIRMED, strongly.** Every Cohort 2 district with retrievable MS documents teaches integrated science in T2: St. Johns Gr 6 mixes energy/forces (physics) and matter/periodic table (chemistry) inside one district-paced year; Irvine's Gr 7–8 are explicitly the *integrated* CA NGSS model (Gr 8: Motion & Forces, Energy, Waves alongside Heredity/Evolution); Chandler requires a generic "Science" credit in each of Gr 7 and Gr 8; Eanes offers IPC as a Gr 8 science option. No Cohort 2 district retrieved runs standalone physics or chemistry courses in middle school. [Inferred from the documents in §2.1, §2.2, §2.5, §2.6]
2. **HS physics/chemistry placement — CONFIRMED with one structural variant.** The Cohort 1 pattern (biology first, chemistry and physics as separate later courses) holds in Chandler (any three of Biology/Chemistry/Physics/Earth Science as separate lab credits) and in Eanes Option 2 (Bio Gr 9 → Chem Gr 10 → Physics Gr 11). The variant: Eanes Option 1 and Irvine both insert an **integrated bridge** — Eanes IPC at Gr 10 before standalone Chemistry (Gr 11) and Physics (Gr 12); Irvine's "Chemistry in the Earth System" → "Physics in the Universe," with chemistry as a stated prerequisite for physics. So the sequence order **Biology → Chemistry → Physics** is consistent across the cohort wherever the order is specified; what varies is whether an integrated course precedes or frames the standalone pair. [Inferred from §2.2, §2.5, §2.6]

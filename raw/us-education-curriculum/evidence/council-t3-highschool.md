Lane 4 shows today's band is `10–18` with **no per-item grade granularity inside it** — T2 vs T3 supply cannot be separated from existing records without re-tagging all 60 items in that band (Lane 4, §d) [Tested]. Reading those 60 items against HS anchors, most are middle-school depth:

A T3 shelf stocked by relabelled T2 items is the single biggest design risk in this tier.

## 1. T3 CALIBRATION RULINGS — per category

**The T3 dial [Inferred from Lane 1, §6]:** STEM difficulty = **formal quantification**; Social Studies difficulty = **verb escalation** (evaluate / critique complex causation); Journals difficulty = **text complexity + independent inquiry**. Formal formulas are **in scope and required** at T3 — a T3 case with no calculable claim, where the concept has one, is under-calibrated.

### 1.1 Math — T3

**Belongs at T3 (core):**
- **Formal functions — YES, core, this is their home.** Functions are formalised in Grade 8 ("each input exactly one output") and then become a high-school **conceptual category** in CCSS (Lane 1, §1a/§1d) [Moderate/Low]. T3 Math is organised by conceptual category — Number & Quantity, Algebra, **Functions**, Geometry, Statistics & Probability, Modeling distributed (Lane 1, §1a) [Moderate 1/1] — **not** by single grade: CCSS HS assigns no topics to Gr 9/10/11/12 individually (Lane 1, §1e) [Inferred from pack].
- Content spine [Inferred]: function notation, domain/range, transformations; linear → quadratic → exponential functions as models; systems of equations; formal trigonometry (ratios → unit-circle extension); coordinate geometry incl. distance/Pythagoras **as proof-and-application**, not first exposure; descriptive statistics, probability, and data-based argument; modeling throughout.
- **Depth rule [Inferred]:** every T3 Math case must require the student to *build or use a model and interpret its parameters* (slope = rate a teen pays/earns, intercept = fixed cost, exponent = growth), not merely execute a procedure. Procedure-only items already inventoried (`standard-algorithm`, `two-digit-divisors`, fraction operations) are **T2, cut from T3** (see §3).
- **Ceiling split [Inferred from Lane 1, §1d]:** unmarked CCSS standards = T3 core; standards marked **(+)** = advanced-course preparation = **T3-extension / T4 bridge**, labelled as such. Same two-level ceiling logic as AP (§2).
- **Sequencing [Inferred]:** publish **two orderings** over one concept-strand spine — traditional Algebra I → Geometry → Algebra II (TX & NY documents; Lane 2, D7) [High 3/3] and Integrated Math I/II/III (CA co-equal pathway; Lane 2, §3a) [Moderate 1/1]. District practice fixes the default path: Algebra 1 in Gr 9 in NYCPS, LAUSD, CPS, and Miami-Dade alike (Lane 3, cross-district table) [Moderate–High].

**Reuse from inventory [Tested]:** `trig:ratios`, `trig:missing-side`, `trig:missing-angle`, `geometry:pythagoras`, `geometry:distance-points`, `rates:linear-model`, `geometry:cylinder-volume` are legitimate T3 seeds — each needs escalation from "find the missing value" to "model, justify, interpret."

### 1.2 Physics — T3

**Belongs at T3 (core), all quantified:**
- **Fnet = ma — T3 core, quantified.** HS-PS2-1: Newton's second law as "the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration," assessment limited to **one-dimensional, macroscopic, non-relativistic** motion (Lane 1, §2e) [Moderate 1/1]. T2 stops at evidence that motion change *depends on* sum of forces + mass (MS-PS2-2) — the equation itself must **not** be a T2 core expectation (Lane 1, §2e, design rule) [Inferred].
- **g-style quantification — T3 core.** Weight (mg), gravitational potential energy (mgh), and projectile/drop problems using a stated gravitational acceleration belong at T3 as direct applications of HS-PS2/HS-PS3 quantification [Inferred]. *Rigor note:* the pack anchors the **placement** (HS-PS2-1/PS2-4, Lane 1, §2e) but does not itself source a numeric value for g — **numeric value: Data not available in the pack**; the curriculum team must source it from the adopted course text, not from this memo.
- **Gravitation + Coulomb's Law — T3 core, mathematical.** HS-PS2-4 extends to mathematical representations of both laws for two-object systems (Lane 1, §2e) [Moderate 1/1]. This is the T3 home of "why phones/satellites/charged objects" quantification — inverse-square reasoning, not just "closer = stronger" (the current `magnet:closer` item is T2).
- **Energy — T3 quantified conservation.** HS endpoint: "energy is a quantitative property of a system… total energy conserved" (Lane 1, §2c, PS3.A) [Moderate 1/1]. KE quantification builds on the T2 endpoint KE ∝ mass × speed² (MS-PS3-1; Lane 1, §2e) [Moderate 1/1] — at T3 students **calculate** with that proportionality (e.g., doubling speed → 4× KE → 4× braking distance) [Inferred]. Waves (HS-PS4): frequency/wavelength/speed relations and information-transfer applications [Inferred from Lane 1, §2d mapping].
- **Nuclear processes (PS1.C / HS-PS1-8 family: fission/fusion/decay) — T3 only**, shared Physics/Chemistry border; never pulled down to T2 (Lane 1, §2f) [Moderate 1/1].

**District reality check:** CPS **requires** 1.0 credit Physics for every graduate, sequenced Gr 9 Biology → Gr 10 Chemistry → **Gr 11 Physics** (Lane 3, §3b) [High 2/2]. Miami-Dade's recommended sequence places Physics I as the 4th-year course (Lane 3, §4b) [Low 1/1]. NYC Regents Physics exists with school-varying placement (Lane 3, §1b) [Moderate 2/2]. **Ruling [Inferred]:** tag T3 Physics by **readiness (Algebra I complete)**, not by a single grade — Gr 11 is the modal placement, not a rule.

### 1.3 Chemistry — T3

**Belongs at T3 (core):**
- **Atomic substructure + periodic system — T3 core.** HS-PS1-1: each atom has charged substructure (protons/neutrons/electrons); periodic table ordered by number of protons (Lane 1, §2c, PS1.A) [Moderate 1/1]. T1 explicitly does not define unseen particles; T2 stops at atoms→molecules + characteristic properties (MS-PS1). Anything sub-atomic in T2 is ahead of the evidence.
- **The mole — T3 core, BOUNDED.** HS-PS1-7 appears **only at HS**: "the mole as the conversion from the atomic to the macroscopic scale," emphasis on conservation of atoms/mass, **"not on memorization and rote application of problem-solving techniques,"** assessment boundary **excludes complex chemical reactions** (Lane 1, §2e) [Moderate 1/1]. NGSS makes **no standalone rote-stoichiometry PE** (Lane 1, §2e) [Moderate 1/1].
  - **Therefore [Inferred]:** T3 core mole work = mass ↔ moles ↔ particle-count conversion **in service of a conservation claim** in simple reactions (e.g., "the rust weighs more — where did the mass come from, in moles?"). Multi-step stoichiometry, limiting-reactant and percent-yield problem sets = **AP extension, labelled beyond-standard** (§2), never T3 core and never a gate for completing T3 Chemistry.
- **Reactions as systems [Inferred]:** balanced equations as atom-accounting (extension of inventoried `reactions:weight`), reaction evidence, and energy in chemical processes (PS3.D) at qualitative-plus-simple-quantitative depth. AP-depth equilibrium/kinetics/thermodynamics/electrochemistry/acid–base treatment is extension only (Lane 1, §4c) [Low 1/1 for the topic list — secondary AP guide, flagged in pack].
- **Misfiled inventory [Inferred]:** `chemistry:water:cycle` is Earth-system content (ESS2.C, roles of water) wearing a Chemistry label — move to GK Earth & Space (§1.4). `reactions:rust`, `states:road-salt`, `mixtures:dissolve` are sound T3 *hooks* but currently pitched at T2 depth; keep the phenomena, rebuild the demand.

### 1.4 General Knowledge — Geography / Earth & Space / Interdisciplinary — T3

**Belongs at T3 (core):** HS-ESS1/2/3 (Lane 1, §2d) [Inferred mapping]: Earth's place in the universe, Earth's systems (plate tectonics, water, weather/climate), Earth & human activity (resources, hazards, climate change). The escalation is real: by end of Grade 12, radioactive decay lifetimes / isotopic content date rock formations and continental rocks can be older than 4 billion years (Lane 1, §2c, ESS1.C) [Low 1/1] — T3 GK must include **deep-time and human-impact argument**, not landmark trivia. Current T3 GK inventory (Everest-vs-Falls, flight time, tower angle) does not touch any of this [Inferred].

**RULING — Earth & Space T3 home: DUAL HOME, by design [Inferred].**
- **Primary shelf = General Knowledge**, as a standalone T3 strand. Evidence: TX names **Earth Systems Science** as a standalone HS course (§112.49) plus Astronomy (Lane 2, §1a/D1) [Moderate 1/1]; NY has a Regents examination/course in Earth and Space Sciences at HS (Lane 2, §2a) [Moderate 2/2]; Miami-Dade leads its recommended HS sequence with **standalone Earth/Space Science in Gr 9** (Lane 3, §4b) [Low 1/1].
- **Woven thread = cross-tags inside Physics and Chemistry cases**, because CA's preferred model **deliberately has no standalone ESS course** — ESS is distributed into *The Living Earth*, *Chemistry in the Earth System*, and *Physics of the Universe* (Lane 2, §3a/D1) [High 2/2], and LAUSD's documented HS maps use exactly those three integrated titles (Lane 3, §2b) [Moderate 1/1].
- A single-home design fails one bloc or the other (Lane 2, D1 design consequence) [Inferred]. Meridian case metadata therefore needs an `earthSpace: true` thread tag independent of category shelf [Inferred].

**Geography split (resolving Lane 1, §3d flag) [Inferred]:** physical/spatial Earth content → GK; human/population/political geography → Social Studies (C3 D2.Geo is single-homed nationally; Meridian's double home must be a deliberate split, not duplication). AP Human Geography caps the Social Studies side (§2).

### 1.5 Social Studies — History / Civics / Economics / Human Geography — T3

**Belongs at T3 (core):** the convergent HS core all three states require — **U.S. History (1 yr) + Government/Civics (1 semester) + Economics (1 semester)** (Lane 2, D5) [High 3/3] — pitched at C3 9–12 verbs:
- History: **evaluate** how events were shaped by time/place + broader contexts (D2.His.1.9-12); analyse change/continuity (D2.His.2.9-12); analyse **multiple and complex causes/effects** (D2.His.14.9-12); **critique central arguments in secondary works** (D2.His.17.9-12) (Lane 1, §3a) [Low 1/1 — single-source reproduction, codes as cited in pack].
- Geography 9–12: **create** maps with geospatial technology (D2.Geo.1.9-12); use maps/satellite images to explain political/cultural/economic dynamics (D2.Geo.2.9-12); **evaluate** how globalisation + scarce resources drive conflict/cooperation (D2.Geo.11.9-12) (Lane 1, §3a) [Low 1/1].
- Economics 9–12: analyse how **incentives** influence choices with differing costs/benefits by group (D2.Eco.1.9-12) (Lane 1, §3a) [Low 1/1]; D2.Eco.2.9-12 full text (marginal benefit/cost): **Data not available** beyond the fragment in the pack — do not quote it as a full standard.
- Civics 9–12: institutions, participation/deliberation, processes/rules/laws at the 9–12 band (Lane 1, §3a structure) [Low–Moderate].

**World History/Geography must be modular [Inferred from Lane 2, D5] [High 3/3 for the divergence]:** NY requires **two full years** (Global History & Geography I + II, Gr 9–10); CA requires one year (Gr 10 Modern World) with **Gr 9 as an elective year**; TX requires **one credit of World Geography OR World History** (choice). Build one shared U.S.+Civics+Economics spine; ship World content as a 2-year NY-depth track / 1-year track / TX either-or fork. NYC Passport detail corroborates the NY shape and shows Gr 12 Economics units explicitly including **Personal Finance, Markets, Indicators** (Lane 3, §1c) [Low 1/1].

**RULING — Personal finance: SEPARABLE UNIT, three presentation modes [Inferred from Lane 2, D3].** Personal finance takes three different legal shapes and Meridian must not fuse it permanently into Economics:
| Mode | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Fused** (TX): one 0.5-credit course, ⅔ personal finance + ⅓ economics, interchangeable with pure Economics for graduation (2022) | Lane 2, §1a/D3 [High 2/2] |
| **Standalone** (CA future): separate one-semester course, **may not be combined** with any other course, for pupils graduating 2030–31; can exempt the economics semester | Lane 2, §3a [Moderate 1/1] |
| **Inside-title** (NY): Gr 12 "Economics, the Enterprise System, and Finance" (0.5 unit) — finance inside economics, no separate PFL course in sources searched | Lane 2, §2a/D3 [High 2/2] |

District corroboration for separability: FL/Miami-Dade requires a **0.5-credit standalone** Personal Financial Literacy & Money Management course for students entering Gr 9 in 2023–24+ (Lane 3, §4d) [Moderate 1/1]; CPS makes Financial Education a **graduation requirement delivered as an integrated unit** (Lane 3, §3d) [High 2/2] — i.e., districts also split on fused-vs-standalone. **Design [Inferred]:** one Personal Finance unit-set (budgeting, credit/APR, pay, saving/investing basics, taxes on a first job) with its own case IDs inside Social Studies–Economics, presentable fused, standalone, or embedded. TX HB 27 (2025) phase-in details as adopted TEKS: **Data not available** (Lane 2, §1a) — do not design to it yet.

### 1.6 Journals — applied field notes — T3

**Belongs at T3:** Journals has **no national discipline of its own** (Lane 1, §6.4) [Inferred] and no state names a Journals subject (Lane 2, §6) — it is a **process tier that rises with the other categories**. T3 calibration anchors:
- Reading: CCSS RI grades 9–10 band — by end of Gr 10, read/comprehend literary nonfiction **independently and proficiently at the high end** of the band; text types include historical, scientific, technical, and economic accounts (Lane 1, §1b) [Low 1/1].
- Inquiry: C3 Dimensions 1/3/4 (question → sources/evidence → communicated conclusion / informed action) as the T3 journal arc (Lane 1, §3b) [Inferred mapping].
- Design: HS-ETS1 engineering design is mapped to Journals only as an [Inferred] closest fit (Lane 1, §2d) — **flag:** if Journals stays reading-focused, ETS1 design challenges do not fit cleanly (Lane 1, §2f). T3 Journals ruling [Inferred]: keep Journals as **evidence work** (field observation, data logging, source critique, write-up) and let ETS1-style build-test-optimise live as an occasional Journals *format*, not its definition.
- **Depth rule [Inferred]:** a T3 journal must end in a **claim defended with the student's own collected evidence** (AP Seminar/Research posture, §2), not a summary of a famous person's story. The inventoried `:stretch` records fail this rule by construction — they are the same T1 stories longer (Lane 4, §b) [Tested].

### 1.7 RULING — Standalone Physics/Chemistry vs CA integrated model

**Decision [Inferred]: KEEP Meridian's separate Physics and Chemistry categories — and pay for the decision with aliases + an on-ramp.** The separate-category structure matches the majority course reality: TX names standalone **Chemistry (§112.43), Physics (§112.45), AND Integrated Physics and Chemistry — IPC (§112.44)** (Lane 2, §1a) [High 2/2]; NY delivers Regents Chemistry and Physics courses (Lane 3, §1b) [Moderate 2/2]; CPS requires Chemistry (Gr 10) and Physics (Gr 11) as separate compulsory courses (Lane 3, §3b) [High 2/2]; Miami-Dade's recommended sequence names Chemistry I and Physics I separately (Lane 3, §4b) [Low 1/1]. It does **not** match CA's preferred classroom reality, where there are **no standalone courses named simply "Physics" or "Chemistry"** (Lane 2, D2) [Inferred from High sources].

Required compensations [Inferred]:
1. **Course aliases on every T3 Physics/Chemistry case:** `CA alias: Physics of the Universe` / `CA alias: Chemistry in the Earth System`; GK/Biology-adjacent cases carry `CA alias: The Living Earth` (course titles: Lane 2, §3a [High 2/2]; Lane 3, §2b [Moderate 1/1]). A CA learner searching by the course name on their schedule must land on the right shelf.
2. **IPC on-ramp at early T3:** a combined Integrated Physics & Chemistry entry sequence (TX §112.44 is the only explicit model; Lane 2, D2) [Inferred] — shared matter/energy/force foundations tagged to **both** categories, so a student whose school runs IPC (or CA integrated) is not forced to pick a silo on day one.
3. **Earth-thread cross-tags** (§1.4) on integrated-model topics, so the CA three-course student experiences Meridian as one woven course, not three disconnected shelves.
4. **Do NOT rename the categories** to the CA titles: that would break TX/NY/CPS/Miami findability to fix CA findability. Aliases solve both; renames solve one by breaking the other [Inferred].

## 3. BLOAT CUTS at T3 — and why

All cuts are [Inferred] recommendations against Lane 4 inventory [Tested] and Lane 1 anchors.

1. **Cut arithmetic-procedure items from T3 Math.** `tables:multi-digit`, `division:standard-algorithm`, `division:two-digit-divisors`, `fractions:multiply/divide/add-unlike`, `place:decimal-shifts`, `measure:convert-metric` sit in 10–18 today (Lane 4, §b) but are Gr 5–7 CCSS work. **Why:** they crowd out the functions/modeling spine that *is* T3 (§1.1) and signal to a 16-year-old that Meridian thinks they are 11. Demote to T2; do not delete — they are correctly placed one tier down.
2. **Cut qualitative Physics repeats.** `push:mass` (relational), `magnet:closer`, `mirror:three-ways`, `sound:medium` as currently pitched duplicate T2 MS-PS2/PS3/PS4. **Why:** T3 Physics value is quantification (§1.2); a qualitative T3 shelf is T2 with an older label. Rebuild the same phenomena as calculated cases or demote.
3. **Cut `chemistry:water:cycle` from Chemistry** (move to GK/ESS, §1.3). **Why:** mis-shelved content teaches students the categories are arbitrary — fatal for the alias strategy in §1.7, which depends on categories meaning something.
4. **Cut GK comparison trivia as T3 content.** `everest-vs-falls`, `falls-in-km`-style "which is bigger" items. **Why:** zero contact with HS-ESS1/2/3 (§1.4); T3 GK needs systems, deep time, and human impact. Keep at T1/T2 where scale comparison is a real skill.
5. **Cut Social Studies route-arithmetic from T3.** `route:travel-time`, `route:scale-ratio`, `compare:time-zones` as standalone T3 cases. **Why:** these are T2 map skills; at T3 the same artefacts must carry D2.Geo.2/11-level evaluation (dynamics, globalisation, resources) or they are bloat. Fold the arithmetic *inside* evaluative cases.
6. **Cut Journals `:stretch` variants as a T3 strategy.** 4 of 16 Journals records are stretch variants (Lane 4, §d) [Tested]. **Why:** length is not level. T3 Journals = new inquiries at RI 9–10 complexity (§1.6), not longer versions of a 9-year-old's Lincoln Memorial case. Retire stretch as a tier-filling mechanism at T3 (it may survive at T2 as a bridge — Data not available on its T2 efficacy in the pack).
7. **Cut rote stoichiometry drills and memorisation loads from T3 Chemistry core** (relocate to AP extension, §2). **Why:** NGSS explicitly bounds them out (Lane 1, §2e) [Moderate 1/1]; making them core would misalign Meridian with every NGSS-adopting state to serve one exam.
8. **Do not build a T3 "everything" survey in any category.** HS courses are year-long and deep (CPS's three required lab sciences; NY's two-year Global sequence — Lane 3). **Why [Inferred]:** Meridian's case-file format wins on depth-per-case; a shallow T3 survey duplicates the textbook it is supposed to make meaningful.

## 5. CASE-FILE PROMPTS — T3 COMPLETE [all Inferred]

Format per Meridian's picker: hook question ("Why Do We Learn This?" case file) + real-world situation line + concept taught. **Reuse flag:** ● = extends an inventoried Lane 4 topic · ○ = new topic beyond inventory. All 24 prompts are **[Inferred]** design proposals, calibrated per §1.

### 5.1 Math (Functions / Modeling / Trig / Statistics)
1. ○ **"Why does your car loan cost way more than the sticker price?"** — A first-car loan at a stated APR over 48 months. *Concept: exponential/compound growth vs linear intuition; total cost of credit.*
2. ● **"Why does the ride-share price surge exactly when everyone wants one?"** (extends `rates:linear-model`) — Surge pricing on a rainy Friday night. *Concept: piecewise/linear models, slope as marginal price, interpreting parameters.*
3. ● **"Can you prove the shortcut across the lot really is shorter — and by how much?"** (extends `geometry:pythagoras` / `distance-points`) — Cutting the diagonal vs walking the edges, measured on a phone map. *Concept: Pythagorean theorem + coordinate distance as a verifiable claim.*
4. ○ **"Why does one viral post explode and another die?"** — Follower growth in the first 24 hours of two posts. *Concept: exponential vs linear vs logistic-shaped growth; fitting a model to real data (Statistics & Probability / Modeling).*

### 5.2 Physics (quantified: Fnet = ma, energy, waves)
1. ● **"Why does doubling your speed quadruple your braking distance?"** (rebuilds `ramp:higher-stored` / `energy:transfer` as calculated) — A new driver at 30 vs 60 mph. *Concept: KE ∝ v² (MS-PS3-1 → HS quantification); energy conservation in stopping.*
2. ● **"How much force does your seatbelt actually have to survive?"** (rebuilds `push:mass` / `push:table`) — A crash-test dummy decelerating from highway speed. *Concept: Fnet = ma (HS-PS2-1), one-dimensional; impulse intuition without the calculus.*
3. ○ **"Why does your phone lose signal in the elevator but the radio doesn't?"** — Wavelength vs the metal box you're standing in. *Concept: HS-PS4 waves — frequency/wavelength, EM radiation interacting with materials.*
4. ○ **"Why don't satellites fall down if gravity never switches off?"** — The phone in your hand getting GPS from orbit. *Concept: mathematical gravitation (HS-PS2-4) — inverse-square fall-off, perpetual free-fall as orbit.*

### 5.3 Chemistry (substructure, mole-bounded, reactions)
1. ● **"Why is the rusty gate heavier than the gate was?"** (rebuilds `reactions:rust` / `reactions:weight` at HS depth) — Weighing a rusted bike lock vs a new one. *Concept: conservation of atoms/mass (HS-PS1-7) with mole-as-conversion — the extra mass is oxygen, counted, not magic.*
2. ○ **"Why does the periodic table have that weird shape?"** — Finding your phone's elements (lithium, silicon, cobalt) on the table. *Concept: HS-PS1-1 — proton number orders the table; substructure explains the pattern.*
3. ● **"Why does road salt melt ice here but fail in a deep freeze?"** (extends `states:road-salt`) — A Midwestern January vs a mild coastal winter. *Concept: particles, freezing-point behaviour, and the limits of a model — when the explanation stops working.*
4. ○ **"How many atoms are in the aluminium can you're holding?"** — One drinks can, one balance, one mole conversion. *Concept: the mole as atomic→macroscopic conversion (HS-PS1-7, bounded — no rote stoichiometry drill).*

### 5.4 General Knowledge — Earth & Space / Geography / Interdisciplinary
1. ○ **"How do scientists know a rock is 4 billion years old if nobody was there?"** — Dating a canyon layer / a meteorite fragment. *Concept: ESS1.C HS endpoint — radioactive decay + isotopic content as a clock (Lane 1, §2c).*
2. ○ **"Why does your city flood in rain that your grandparents' city shrugged off?"** — A local flash-flood news clip from the student's own region. *Concept: HS-ESS2/ESS3 — water in Earth's systems + human impacts (pavement, drainage) as one system.*
3. ● **"Why is the shortest flight path on the map the curved one?"** (extends `mixed:paris-sydney-flight`; note `mixed:great-circle` already exists at 18+ — **reuse/downshift candidate, flag for T4 seat**) — Watching a flight tracker bend over the pole. *Concept: spherical Earth geometry + ESS1.B patterns; flat maps distort.*
4. ○ **"Why is your phone's weather app always a little bit wrong about your street?"** — Forecast says dry; your block gets soaked. *Concept: HS-ESS2.D weather/climate modelling — resolution, data, and why models have error bars.*

### 5.5 Social Studies — History / Civics / Economics / Human Geography
1. ○ **"Why did your first paycheck come out smaller than your hourly wage says it should?"** — A part-time job's first pay stub. *Concept: Economics + personal finance (separable unit, §1.5) — gross vs net, withholding, what the deductions fund (civics link).*
2. ○ **"Why does a minimum-wage rise help one worker and cost another their hours?"** — Two friends, same town, opposite outcomes after a wage change. *Concept: D2.Eco.1.9-12 — incentives and costs/benefits that differ by group; marginal thinking (full D2.Eco.2.9-12 text: Data not available).*
3. ○ **"Who actually decides whether your school day starts earlier?"** — A school-board vote on start times / a city curfew debate. *Concept: Civics 9–12 — institutions, levels of government, participation: where the decision legally lives and how a citizen acts on it (C3 Dimensions 1/4).*
4. ● **"Why are two neighbourhoods in the same city living in different centuries?"** (extends `region:density` to D2.Geo.2/11 level) — Satellite images + population density of the student's own metro area. *Concept: Human Geography 9–12 — spatial patterns, economic dynamics, resource access; evaluate, don't just describe.*

### 5.6 Journals — applied field notes (process tier)
1. ● **"Can you prove your school's Wi-Fi dead zones are where everyone says they are?"** (extends `technology:mobile-phone`) — A week of signal logging on the student's own phone, mapped by hand. *Concept: ETS1-style problem definition + SEP data collection; claim defended with own evidence (§1.6).*
2. ○ **"What does your street sound like at 7am vs 7pm — in numbers?"** — Phone decibel logging across a school week. *Concept: field measurement, sampling choices, and honest error — RI 9–10 technical-text reading + evidence sorting.*
3. ● **"Can you re-measure the Earth with a stick, like Eratosthenes did?"** (extends `people-places:eratosthenes-earth`) — Partner-school shadow measurements on the same day, compared live. *Concept: replication as a journal method — historical case re-performed, not just retold; geometry in service of evidence.*
4. ○ **"Write the field note that would convince a sceptic your local creek is changing."** — Three visits, photos, simple counts, one defended claim. *Concept: AP Seminar posture — question, source evaluation, communicated conclusion (C3 Dimensions 1/3/4); the T3 Journals ceiling in miniature.*

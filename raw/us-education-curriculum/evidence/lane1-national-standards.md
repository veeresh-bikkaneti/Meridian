# Lane 1 — U.S. National Standards / Frameworks
## Evidence file for Meridian 4-tier curriculum design
**Date:** 2026-11-24  
**Scope:** Research only. U.S. national standards/frameworks only.  
**Meridian categories in scope:** Math; Physics; Chemistry; General Knowledge (Geography / Earth & Space / Interdisciplinary); Social Studies (History / Civics / Economics / Human Geography); Journals (applied field notes / real-world case studies).  
**Meridian tiers:** T1 Upper Elementary ages 7–10 (Grades 2–5); T2 Middle School ages 11–14 (Grades 6–8); T3 High School ages 14–18 (Grades 9–12); T4 College & Adult 18+.

> **How to read confidence tags:** Every sourced claim ends with a direct source link + tag. [High x/y] = 3+ agreeing primary sources; [Moderate x/y] = 2 agreeing or 1 primary; [Low x/y] = 1 secondary or conflicting. Interpretations by this researcher are labelled [Inferred]. Unverifiable items are written as "Data not available" and dropped — no standard code, grade placement, or figure is invented.

> **Band-alignment note [Inferred]:** National bands do not line up exactly with Meridian tiers. Common Core is grade-by-grade K–8; NGSS uses K–2 / 3–5 / 6–8 / 9–12 performance expectations with Framework endpoints by end of grades 2, 5, 8, 12; C3 uses K–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12. Meridian T1 (Grades 2–5) therefore straddles the K–2 / 3–5 boundary in NGSS and C3 — T1 content for Grade 2 should use the K–2 endpoint, Grades 3–5 the 3–5 endpoint. This straddle is a design decision point, not a national standard.

## 1. Common Core State Standards (CCSS) — Mathematics, and ELA for Journals

### 1a. Grade-band structure

- CCSS Mathematics is **organized by grade level in Grades K–8**, and at high school **by conceptual category** — "number and quantity, algebra, functions, geometry, modeling and probability and statistics" — showing the body of knowledge to be college and career ready. Source: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED522006 [Moderate 1/1 — 1 primary-origin record (ERIC record of CCSS Initiative Appendix A)]
- The same structure is stated as: "K-8 grade-by-grade standards organized by domain; 9-12 high school standards organized by conceptual categories." Source: https://ces.rocklinusd.org/documents/CCCS/Understanding%20the%20Common%20Core%20State%20Standards.pdf [Moderate 2/2 — agrees with ERIC record above; republished CCSS presentation, counted as corroboration not a second origin]
- **K–5 foundation:** "The K-5 standards provide students with a solid foundation in whole numbers, addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, fractions and decimals." Source: https://ces.rocklinusd.org/documents/CCCS/Understanding%20the%20Common%20Core%20State%20Standards.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- **Grades 6–8:** "The 6-8 standards describe robust learning in geometry, algebra, and probability and statistics" and "the standards for grades 7 and 8 include significant algebra and geometry content." Source: https://ces.rocklinusd.org/documents/CCCS/Understanding%20the%20Common%20Core%20State%20Standards.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- **High-school conceptual categories — two listings in sources, treated as one origin family:** (i) five categories listed as "Number and Quantity, Algebra, Functions, Geometry, and Statistics and Probability" with "Modeling standards are distributed under the five"; (ii) six listed when Modeling is counted separately: "Number and Quantity, Algebra, Functions, Modeling, Geometry, Statistics and Probability." Both wordings appear in the same CCSS presentation set. Source: https://ces.rocklinusd.org/documents/CCCS/Understanding%20the%20Common%20Core%20State%20Standards.pdf [Moderate 1/1 — internal variation noted, not a conflict between independent origins]
- **Domains in Grades 6–8 (corroborating secondary synthesis of CCSS):** Ratios and Proportional Relationships; The Number System; Expressions and Equations; Geometry; Statistics and Probability; and Functions introduced in Grade 8. Source: https://pressbooks.palomar.edu/schoolagecurriculum/chapter/middle-school-math-standards/ [Low 1/1 — 1 secondary, consistent with CCSS domain names in primary presentation]
- **Grade-level topic landing [Inferred synthesis of the sources above + fraction progression below]:**
  - **Grades 2–5 (T1):** Operations and Algebraic Thinking; Number and Operations in Base Ten; Number and Operations — Fractions (from Grade 3); Measurement and Data; Geometry. Fractions progression is explicit: Grade 3 — fraction as number on number line, equivalence in special cases; Grade 4 — equivalence a/b = (n×a)/(n×b) via visual models, addition with like denominators; Grade 5 — add/subtract fractions with unlike denominators via equivalent fractions. Source: https://www.thecorestandards.org/assets/2010_6_10_WU.pdf [Moderate 1/1 — thecorestandards.org-hosted expert presentation of CCSS text]
  - **Grades 6–8 (T2):** Grade 6 — ratios/unit rates, dividing fractions, negative numbers, first variable expressions (6.EE); Grade 7 — proportional relationships, rational-number operations, multi-step equations; Grade 8 — formal definition of function, linear equations/systems, Pythagorean theorem, irrational numbers, exponents/scientific notation. The Grade 6/7/8 sequencing detail in this bullet is from secondary syntheses, not quoted CCSS text. Sources: https://pressbooks.palomar.edu/schoolagecurriculum/chapter/middle-school-math-standards/ and https://www.nivelera.com/blog/middle-school-ccss-algebra-readiness-grades-6-8 [Low 2/2 — 2 agreeing secondary sources; verify against full CCSS PDF before publishing standard codes]
  - **Grades 9–12 (T3):** No grade-by-grade split in CCSS itself — content is by conceptual category (above), sequenced by states/districts into courses (traditional Algebra I → Geometry → Algebra II, or integrated). The "by course, not by grade" point is stated in secondary synthesis. Source: https://sciencewithshobha.com/blog/us-math-syllabus-grade-by-grade-k-to-12-what-each-grade-teaches-and-the-cbse-class-it-matches/ [Low 1/1]

### 1b. ELA — only as it touches Journals / reading informational text

- Reading: Informational Text (RI) is a distinct CCSS ELA strand at every grade. Example Grade 3: RI.3.1 "Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text"; RI.3.3 describe relationship between historical events / scientific ideas / steps in technical procedures using time, sequence, cause/effect language; RI.3.5 use text features/search tools; RI.3.10 by end of year read/comprehend informational texts "including history/social studies, science, and technical texts, at the high end of the grades 2-3 text complexity band." Source: https://portal.ct.gov/SDE/CT-Core-Standards/Materials-for-Teachers/ELA/ELA-Units/ELA/Grade-3-Reading-Informational-Texts---The-Declaration-of-Independence-and-the-First-Fourth-of-July [Moderate 1/1 — state education agency reproducing CCSS codes/text]
- Example Grade 7: RI.7.1 cite several pieces of textual evidence; RI.7.2 determine two or more central ideas and provide objective summary; RI.7.8 trace/evaluate argument and claims, assessing reasoning and evidence; RI.7.10 read/comprehend literary nonfiction in grades 6–8 text complexity band. Source: https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/Education_Resources/standards-alignment/frc/commoncore2122FTCCPFRC.pdf?hsLang=en [Low 1/1 — 1 secondary alignment document reproducing CCSS text]
- Example Grades 9–10: RI.9-10.10 — by end of Grade 9 read/comprehend literary nonfiction in grades 9–10 band with scaffolding as needed; by end of Grade 10 independently/proficiently at high end. Informational text types listed include historical, scientific, technical, economic accounts written for a broad audience, plus essays, speeches, biographies/memoirs, journalism. Source: https://www.shmoop.com/common-core-standards/ccss-ela-literacy-ri-9-10-10.html/english-language-arts-language-anchor-standards.html [Low 1/1 — 1 secondary]
- **Relevance to Journals [Inferred]:** CCSS does **not** create a "journal" subject. Journals maps to RI (reading informational/technical text) + Writing W.3.8-type expectations (recall/gather information, take brief notes, sort evidence — quoted in the Grade 3 source above). Meridian Journals should therefore be calibrated as *applied informational reading + note-taking / evidence-sorting*, escalating by text-complexity band (2–3, 4–5, 6–8, 9–10, 11–CCR), not as a separate national discipline. Source for W.3.8 wording: https://portal.ct.gov/SDE/CT-Core-Standards/Materials-for-Teachers/ELA/ELA-Units/ELA/Grade-3-Reading-Informational-Texts---The-Declaration-of-Independence-and-the-First-Fourth-of-July [Moderate 1/1]

### 1d. Difficulty-calibration anchors — CCSS Math

- **Fractions are a multi-year ramp, not a T1 one-shot:** Grade 3 = fraction as point on number line; Grade 4 = equivalence + like-denominator addition; Grade 5 = unlike-denominator addition/subtraction. Placing formal unlike-denominator work before Grade 5 would be ahead of CCSS. Source: https://www.thecorestandards.org/assets/2010_6_10_WU.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- **Formal algebra starts in T2, not T1:** Expressions & Equations spans Grades 6–8 and "mark a shift from arithmetic to algebra" — variable notation in Grade 6 (6.EE), multi-step linear equations in Grade 7, systems/linear functions in Grade 8. Source: https://pressbooks.palomar.edu/schoolagecurriculum/chapter/middle-school-math-standards/ [Low 1/1 — secondary wording, domain placement consistent with CCSS presentation]
- **Functions are formalised in Grade 8, then become a high-school conceptual category.** Grade 8: function as rule assigning each input exactly one output. Source: https://pressbooks.palomar.edu/schoolagecurriculum/chapter/middle-school-math-standards/ [Low 1/1]
- **"+" standards = advanced-course preparation:** High-school standards marked (+) are additional mathematics for advanced courses such as calculus / advanced statistics / discrete mathematics; unmarked standards are the common college-and-career-ready curriculum. Source: https://ces.rocklinusd.org/documents/CCCS/Understanding%20the%20Common%20Core%20State%20Standards.pdf [Moderate 1/1] — **Use for T3 ceiling design [Inferred]:** core T3 = unmarked; T3-extension / T4 bridge = (+) content.

### 1e. Gaps / flags — CCSS

- CCSS covers **only Math and ELA** nationally in this lane — it provides no Physics, Chemistry, or Social Studies content standards. Those come from NGSS and C3 respectively. [Inferred from framework scope in sources above]
- High-school CCSS Math does **not** assign topics to Grades 9, 10, 11, 12 individually — any Meridian T3 sub-banding by single grade is a Meridian design choice, not a CCSS requirement. [Inferred]

## 2. NGSS — Next Generation Science Standards

### 2a. Grade-band structure

- NGSS were "released in 2013" through a "collaborative, state-led process," are "rich in both content and practice, and arranged in a coherent manner across disciplines and grades," and integrate three dimensions from the NRC *Framework for K-12 Science Education* into performance expectations: Science & Engineering Practices (SEPs), Disciplinary Core Ideas (DCIs), Crosscutting Concepts (CCCs). Source: https://www.nextgenscience.org/understanding-standards/understanding-standards [Moderate 1/1 — official NGSS site]
- **Framework grade-band endpoints** are defined "by the end of grades 2, 5, 8, and 12" for each DCI. Source: https://aaas-arise.org/resource/framework-for-k-12-science-education-practices-crosscutting-concepts-and-core-ideas/ [Low 1/1 — secondary summary of the Framework, consistent with Appendix E sources below]
- NGSS Appendix E organises every DCI progression in columns **K–2 | 3–5 | 6–8 | 9–12**. Worked example PS1.A (Structure and Properties of Matter) shows that exact 4-column progression. Source: https://www.sdcoe.net/ngss/dimensions-of-the-ngss/disciplinary-core-ideas/dci-physical-sciences/ps1a-structure-and-properties-of-matter [Moderate 1/1 — county office of education reproducing Appendix E verbatim; labelled "from NGSS Appendix E"]
- **Performance expectations are coded by band:** elementary by single grade (e.g., 3-PS2, 4-PS3, 5-PS1), middle school as MS- (Grades 6–8), high school as HS- (Grades 9–12), engineering in elementary as 3-5-ETS1. Example list for Grades 3–5 assessment includes 3-PS2, 5-PS1, 3-LS, 3-ESS, 4-ESS, 3-5-ETS1 codes side by side. Source: https://osse.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/osse/publication/attachments/DC%20Science%20Assessment%20Design%20and%20Blueprints%20Grade%205%2010.29.18.pdf [Low 1/1 — district document reproducing NGSS codes]

### 2c. What sits in each band — worked progressions (verified examples)

- **PS1.A Matter:** K–2 — matter described/classified by observable properties, solid/liquid depending on temperature (2-PS1-1); 3–5 — matter subdivided into particles too small to see, amount (weight) conserved when it changes form (5-PS1-2), properties used to identify materials, with explicit boundary "mass and weight are not distinguished, and no attempt is made to define the unseen particles" (5-PS1-3); 6–8 — substances made from different types of atoms combining in various ways, molecules from two to thousands of atoms (MS-PS1-1), each pure substance has characteristic properties (MS-PS1-2/3); 9–12 — each atom has charged substructure (nucleus of protons/neutrons surrounded by electrons, HS-PS1-1), periodic table ordered by number of protons. Source: https://www.sdcoe.net/ngss/dimensions-of-the-ngss/disciplinary-core-ideas/dci-physical-sciences/ps1a-structure-and-properties-of-matter [Moderate 1/1]
- **PS3.A Energy:** K–2 — N/A / intentionally blank endpoint; 3–5 — "the faster a given object is moving, the more energy it possesses," energy moved by moving objects, sound, light, electric currents (4-PS3-1/2), boundary: "no attempt is made to give a precise or complete definition of energy"; 6–8 — kinetic energy "proportional to the mass of the moving object and grows with the square of its speed," potential energy from relative positions, temperature = measure of average kinetic energy of particles (MS-PS3-1/2/3/4); 9–12 — "energy is a quantitative property of a system that depends on the motion and interactions of matter and radiation within that system," total energy conserved. Source: https://www.sdcoe.net/ngss/dimensions-of-the-ngss/disciplinary-core-ideas/dci-physical-sciences/ps3a-definitions-of-energy [Moderate 1/1]
- **ESS1.C History of Planet Earth (illustrates ESS escalation):** by end Grade 2 — some Earth events are cycles, some quick, some very slow; by end Grade 5 — landforms/weathering/erosion infer landscape history, fossil location indicates order of rock layers; by end Grade 8 — geological time scale from rock strata organises Earth's history; by end Grade 12 — radioactive decay lifetimes/isotopic content date rock formations, continental rocks can be older than 4 billion years. Source: https://aae.lewiscenter.org/documents/AAE/Science/NGSS/framework_chapter_7.pdf [Low 1/1 — Framework chapter PDF via school host]

### 2e. Difficulty-calibration anchors — NGSS

- **Newton's laws — qualitative → relational → quantitative:**
  - Elementary: pushes/pulls, balanced/unbalanced forces qualitatively (3-PS2-1/2 family; K-PS2 pushes/pulls in CA listing). Source: https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/science?c0=25%2C9%2C8%2C30%2C37%2C28%2C24%2C41%2C37%2C8&c2=3 [Low 1/1]
  - Middle school MS-PS2-2: "Plan an investigation to provide evidence that the change in an object's motion depends on the sum of the forces on the object and the mass of the object" — Newton's 1st/2nd law relationship **without** the formal equation as the assessed claim; MS-PS2-1 applies Newton's 3rd law to colliding objects. Source: https://www.fizziq.org/en/ngss/ [Low 1/1 — secondary table quoting NGSS PE wording verbatim] corroborated by https://www.singingscientific.com/newton-questions-1 [Low 1/1]
  - High school HS-PS2-1: "Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration" (Fnet = ma), assessment limited to one-dimensional motion, macroscopic, non-relativistic. Source: https://static.nsta.org/ngss/20130509/dci-individual/HS-PS2-MotionAndStability-ForcesAndInteractions.pdf [Moderate 1/1 — NSTA-hosted NGSS PE document]
  - HS-PS2-4 extends to mathematical representations of Newton's Law of Gravitation and Coulomb's Law (two-object systems). Same source as HS-PS2-1 above. [Moderate 1/1]
  - **Design rule [Inferred]:** Do not place quantified F = ma problem-solving in T1 or T2 as a core expectation — T2 is evidence-for-dependence (force + mass), T3 is the quantified law.
- **Mole concept — appears only at high school, in HS-PS1-7:** "Use mathematical representations to support the claim that atoms, and therefore mass, are conserved during a chemical reaction." Clarification: emphasis on "the translation of these relationships to the macroscopic scale using the mole as the conversion from the atomic to the macroscopic scale" and "not on memorization and rote application of problem-solving techniques"; assessment boundary: "does not include complex chemical reactions." Source: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/documents/ngsshsphyicalscidci.pdf [Moderate 1/1 — CA Dept. of Education NGSS HS Physical Science by DCI] corroborated by https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/science?c0=37%2C4%2C1%2C31%2C32%2C24%2C6%2C26%2C32%2C6%2C22%2C11%2C9%2C20%2C13%2C1%2C21%2C39%2C33%2C12&c1=5%2C1%2C5%2C1&c3=4&page=1 [Low 1/1]
  - NGSS's own stoichiometry note frames stoichiometry as integrable "when building towards performance expectations that address chemical reactions and conservation of atoms" — i.e., NGSS does **not** make rote stoichiometry a standalone PE. Source: https://www.nextgenscience.org/commonly-searched-terms/stoichiometry-0 [Moderate 1/1 — official NGSS site]
  - **Design rule [Inferred]:** Mole / Avogadro-scale conversion is a **T3 Chemistry ceiling anchor**, not T2. T2 Chemistry stops at atoms→molecules and characteristic properties (MS-PS1). Full AP-level stoichiometry (limiting reactant, percent yield) goes beyond NGSS HS-PS1-7's stated boundary — see §4.
- **Kinetic energy quantification is T2:** KE proportional to mass and to square of speed is the end-of-Grade-8 endpoint (MS-PS3-1), while T1 (Grade 4) is only "faster = more energy." Source: https://www.sdcoe.net/ngss/dimensions-of-the-ngss/disciplinary-core-ideas/dci-physical-sciences/ps3a-definitions-of-energy [Moderate 1/1]
- **Atomic substructure is T3:** protons/neutrons/electrons and periodic-table-by-proton-number are HS-PS1-1 (9–12) in the PS1.A progression; T1 explicitly does **not** define unseen particles. Source: https://www.sdcoe.net/ngss/dimensions-of-the-ngss/disciplinary-core-ideas/dci-physical-sciences/ps1a-structure-and-properties-of-matter [Moderate 1/1]

### 2f. Gaps / flags — NGSS

- **⚠️ LIFE SCIENCE HAS NO MERIDIAN HOME — major gap.** LS1–LS4 is one of NGSS's three science disciplines, present in every band (e.g., 3-LS1/LS2/LS3/LS4, MS-LS1–4, HS-LS1–4), but Meridian's 6 categories contain no Biology / Life Science category. Options are a design decision for the parent synthesis: (a) add a category, (b) house LS explicitly inside General Knowledge — Interdisciplinary, or (c) declare LS out of scope and accept that Meridian cannot claim NGSS completeness. This file takes no position. DCI list source: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/documents/ngssataglance.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- **Engineering Design (ETS1) also has no dedicated Meridian category.** It is mapped to Journals above only as an [Inferred] closest fit. If Journals is meant to be reading-focused (per CCSS RI), ETS1 design challenges will not fit cleanly — flag for synthesis.
- **Nuclear processes (PS1.C, HS-PS1-8 fission/fusion/decay)** sit inside Chemistry/Physics at T3 only — do not pull down into T2. PE family referenced in: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/documents/ngsshsphyicalscidci.pdf [Moderate 1/1]

## 3. C3 Framework — College, Career, and Civic Life (Social Studies)

### 3a. Structure

- Published 2013 by National Council for the Social Studies (NCSS): "The College, Career and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social Studies State Standards." It "focuses on the four core disciplines of social studies — civics, economics, geography, and history — with appendices on psychology, sociology, and anthropology, and a supplement on Religious Studies." Source: https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2021-12/se-85062021332.pdf [Moderate 1/1 — official socialstudies.org journal article describing the Framework]
- Full citation: National Council for the Social Studies (NCSS), *The College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social Studies State Standards: Guidance for Enhancing the Rigor of K-12 Civics, Economics, Geography, and History* (Silver Spring, MD: NCSS, 2013). Source: https://archive.org/stream/c-3-framework-for-social-studies/C3-Framework-for-Social-Studies_djvu.txt [Low 1/1 — full-text republication of the NCSS document; one origin]
- **The Inquiry Arc — four Dimensions:**
  1. Developing Questions and Planning Inquiries
  2. Applying Disciplinary Concepts and Tools
  3. Evaluating Sources and Using Evidence
  4. Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action
  
  Source: https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2021-12/se-85062021332.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- **Grade bands:** "The Framework sets objectives for each of four grade bands (K-2, 3-5, 6-8, and 9-12)." Source: https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2021-12/se-85062021332.pdf [Moderate 1/1]
- **Dimension 2 is organised by discipline**, with indicator codes D2.Civ.*, D2.Eco.*, D2.Geo.*, D2.His.*, each suffixed by band (e.g., .K-2, .3-5, .6-8, .9-12). Subsections verified in sources:
  - Civics: Civic and Political Institutions; Participation and Deliberation (Applying Civic Virtues and Democratic Principles); Processes, Rules, and Laws. Source: https://www.ixl.com/social-studies/skill-plans/vermont-college-career-and-civic-life-3-framework-grade-1.pdf [Low 1/1 — alignment document reproducing C3 indicator text/codes]
  - Economics: Economic Decision Making; Exchange and Markets; The National Economy; The Global Economy. Same IXL source above for K–2 indicators (D2.Eco.1.K-2 scarcity necessitates decision making; D2.Eco.14.K-2 why people trade across countries). [Low 1/1]
  - Geography: Geographic Representations: Spatial Views of the World; Human-Environment Interaction; Human Population: Spatial Patterns and Movements (plus Spatial Patterns / Global Interconnections at 9–12 in Dimension 2 document). Source: https://history.appstate.edu/sites/history.appstate.edu/files/c3_framework_-_dimension_2_-_all_social_studies.pdf [Low 1/1 — university-hosted reproduction of C3 Dimension 2]
  - History: Change, Continuity, and Context; Perspectives; Historical Sources and Evidence; Causation and Argumentation. Source: https://history.appstate.edu/sites/history.appstate.edu/files/c3_framework_-_dimension_2_-_all_social_studies.pdf [Low 1/1]
- **Escalation examples (verified indicator wording):**

## 5. T4 — College & Adult: national general-education / readiness expectations

### 5a. ACT College Readiness Benchmarks (official)

- Definition: "The ACT College Readiness Benchmarks are the ACT scores that represent the level of achievement required for students to have a 50% chance of obtaining a B or higher and about a 75-80% chance of obtaining a C or higher in corresponding credit-bearing courses," established from data from hundreds of U.S. colleges. Source: https://www.act.org/content/act/en/college-and-career-readiness/benchmarks.html [Moderate 1/1 — official act.org]
- **Benchmark table (official):**

| ACT score type | College course(s) | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| English | English Composition I | 18 |
| Mathematics | College Algebra | 22 |
| Reading | American History, Other History, Psychology, Sociology, Political Science, Economics | 22 |
| Science | Biology | 23 |
| STEM | Calculus, Chemistry, Biology, Physics, Engineering | 26 |
| ELA | English Composition I + the Reading-course list above | 20 |

Source: https://www.act.org/content/act/en/college-and-career-readiness/benchmarks.html [Moderate 1/1]
- **T4 implication [Inferred]:** ACT benchmarks define the **floor of T4 / ceiling of T3**, not a T4 curriculum: T4 entry assumes College Algebra readiness (Math 22), first-year science readiness (Science 23 / STEM 26 for Calculus-Chemistry-Physics-Engineering), and social-science reading readiness (Reading 22). Meridian T4 content should start *at* that assumed floor and build general-education depth above it — it should not re-teach T3 content labelled as "college."

### 5b. AAC&U LEAP Essential Learning Outcomes + VALUE Quantitative Literacy (national association, not a government standard)

- AAC&U is described as "the leading national association concerned with the quality, vitality, and public standing of undergraduate liberal education." Its LEAP Essential Learning Outcomes state students should gain: Knowledge of Human Cultures and the Physical and Natural World (through sciences/mathematics, social sciences, humanities, histories, languages, arts); Intellectual and Practical Skills including inquiry and analysis, critical/creative thinking, written/oral communication, **quantitative literacy**, information literacy, teamwork/problem solving; Personal and Social Responsibility including civic knowledge and engagement — local and global; and Integrative and Applied Learning. Source: https://www.in.gov/che/files/5-B_-_Decision_Item_A_Glossary_of_Terms.pdf [Low 1/1 — state agency glossary reproducing AAC&U LEAP] corroborated by https://secure.in.gov/che/files/LEAP.pdf [Low 1/1 — same LEAP one-pager republication; counted as one origin family]
- VALUE Quantitative Literacy rubric: QL "also known as Numeracy or Quantitative Reasoning (QR)"; employers/students need skills such as drawing information from charts/graphs/geometric figures and completing estimations/calculations; QL work involves analysing quantitative information, representing it in appropriate forms, completing calculations to answer meaningful questions, making judgements based on quantitative data, and communicating results. The VALUE rubrics articulate criteria "with performance descriptors demonstrating progressively more sophisticated levels of attainment" and are "intended for institutional-level use in evaluating and discussing student learning, not for grading." Source: https://us.utah.edu/_resources/documents/general-education/rubrics/Quantitative%20Literacy%20Value%20Rubric.pdf [Low 1/1 — university-hosted republication of AAC&U VALUE rubric; AAC&U origin stated in document]
- **Status flag:** AAC&U LEAP/VALUE is a **voluntary national association framework**, not a federal/national standard and not adopted as law. Use it for T4 as a *reference framework*, labelled as such — do not present it as "the national college standard." [Inferred assessment of status from sources above]

## 6. Cross-framework synthesis for the parent (all [Inferred] unless cited)

1. **Three different band logics must be harmonised by Meridian, not assumed identical:** CCSS Math = per-grade K–8 + conceptual categories HS; NGSS = band PEs (single-grade elementary, MS, HS) + Framework endpoints at 2/5/8/12; C3 = end-of-band indicators K–2/3–5/6–8/9–12. Meridian T1 (2–5) cuts across the 2/5 boundary in NGSS/C3/Framework — Grade 2 needs separate handling inside T1.
2. **Quantification is the main difficulty dial in STEM:** T1 = qualitative/observable (forces as pushes/pulls, faster = more energy, matter by observable properties); T2 = relational/proportional (KE ∝ m·v² stated, force–mass–motion dependence as evidence, atoms→molecules); T3 = formal/quantified (Fnet = ma, gravitation/Coulomb mathematically, mole as atomic→macroscopic conversion, periodic system by proton number). Each step is anchored in §1d/§2e.
3. **In Social Studies the dial is the verb, not the topic:** describe/explain (T1) → assess/analyse consequences (T2) → evaluate/critique complex causation and secondary arguments (T3), per C3 indicators in §3a/§3c. AP (§4) then adds college-level depth on the same four C3 disciplines.
4. **Journals has no national discipline of its own.** Its national anchors are process anchors: CCSS RI + note-taking writing, NGSS SEPs/CCCs + ETS1 design, C3 Dimensions 1/3/4 (question → evidence → communicated conclusion/action), AP Seminar/Research at the ceiling. Design Journals as a *process tier that rises with the other categories*, not as a content subject with its own topic ladder. [Inferred]
5. **Two structural gaps need owner/synthesis decisions before curriculum writing:** (a) Life Science (full NGSS discipline + ACT Science benchmark course + AP Biology) has no Meridian category; (b) Geography is split across two Meridian categories while C3/NGSS-ESS/AP Human Geography each treat it differently (C3 D2.Geo = human/spatial; NGSS ESS = physical Earth system; AP Human Geography = human). A single "Geography" ladder built from only one framework will misplace the others' content.

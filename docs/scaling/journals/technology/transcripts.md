# Technology journal — draft transcripts (Stage A)

DRAFT. Every spoken line is drawn from a sourced fact in `sources.md` (fact ids
in brackets). Nothing here is rendered yet. Word counts are counted by hand;
whether each line fits about 8 s of speech is TO CONFIRM when the narration is
rendered in Stage B ("cut, never rush", `docs/journal-movies.md`). The opener is
the recipe's fixed line. Read-aloud covers the opening question and the
retrieval check, which appear in the player card, not in the video frame (the
recipe keeps the frame free of text).

## technology:first-phone (group 7-10)

**Opening question (read aloud, before play):** The first phone call was in
1876. How many years ago was that? *(Answer: 2026 − 1876 = 150 in 2026. The
answer depends on the current year, see open item O6.)* [T1-2]

**Narration (16 spoken words, PROPOSED):**
> Hey kids, have you heard about this place? Boston, 1876. A voice travelled
> down a wire. [T1-2, T1-3, T1-5]

**What the picture shows (for the transcript):** an attic hall with one wire
running along it; a wave moves along the wire; at the far end a second man
listens at the receiver. [T1-3, T1-5, T1-6]

**Retrieval check (read aloud, tap one):** In the first phone call, how did the
voice travel? Choices: *Down a wire* (correct) · *Through the air* · *In a
letter*. Hint on a miss: "Look at the line between the two rooms."

## technology:mobile-phone (group 10-18)

**Opening question (read aloud, before play):** The first phone call down a
wire was in 1876. The first public call from a phone you could carry was in
1973. How many years apart were they? Solve 1876 + x = 1973. *(Answer:
x = 97.)* [T1-2, T2-1]

**Narration (18 spoken words, PROPOSED):**
> Hey kids, have you heard about this place? 1973. First public call from a
> phone you could carry. [T2-1, T2-5]

**What the picture shows:** an engineer in New York City holds a large
handheld phone with no wire; invisible waves are drawn as soft arcs leaving
it. No tower is drawn (T2-U4) and no size comparison is drawn (none is
sourced). [T2-1, T2-3]

**Retrieval check (read aloud, tap one):** What did the 1973 phone not need?
Choices: *A wire* (correct) · *A battery* · *A person to talk to*. Hint on a
miss: "Car phones before it ran on car batteries. What was missing from this
one?" [T2-3, T2-5]

## technology:eniac (group 7-10)

**Opening question (read aloud, before play):** ENIAC, an early computer, was
kept in a basement room 50 feet long and 30 feet wide. How much floor did that
room cover? *(Answer: 50 × 30 = 1,500 square feet.)* [T3-3]

**Narration (18 spoken words, PROPOSED):**
> Hey kids, have you heard about this place? Philadelphia, 1946. ENIAC, an
> early computer, filled a big room. [T3-2, T3-3, T3-6, T3-8]

**What the picture shows:** long rows of tall machine panels around a
basement room, with many small tubes. No people added, no glowing effect, no
modern phone, no military scene. [T3-3, T3-4]

**Retrieval check (read aloud, tap one):** How big was ENIAC?
Choices: *It took up a big room* (correct) · *It fit in a pocket* ·
*It fit on a desk*. Hint on a miss: "Think of the room in the movie, 50 feet
by 30 feet."

**18+ stretch question (read aloud after the retrieval check, owner call O3):** ENIAC could do 5,000 additions a second. How many in one minute? *(Answer: 5,000 × 60 = 300,000.)* [T3-5] Group 18+. Not narrated, so the clip stays at 18 spoken words.

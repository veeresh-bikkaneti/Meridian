Fact ids for contract I5 `stretch.fact`. Accessed 2026-11-10.
Nothing in a film or a check uses a fact that is not on this list.
Confidence: HIGH = a reference work or the museum's own page. MEDIUM = a second-hand page. A conflict is not taught.

## greenwich-zero-1884

The line at Greenwich was set as the world's prime meridian in 1884.
[Royal Museums Greenwich](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/time/what-prime-meridian-why-it-greenwich). HIGH.
The same page describes a vote among delegates. The vote is not taught (§7a).

[geonames.org/6295636](https://www.geonames.org/6295636/royal-greenwich-observatory.html). HIGH.

## clock-model-15-degrees

A full turn is 360 degrees. A clock day has 24 hours. 360 ÷ 24 = 15.
Those two inputs are stated in the stretch question. The answer is the quotient.
This is a clock model, not a claim about the exact spin of the Earth.
A university geography note states the same step: [Geography 101, University of Hawaii](https://laulima.hawaii.edu/access/content/group/dbd544e4-dcdd-4631-b8ad-3304985e1be2/book/chapter_1/time.htm). MEDIUM for the Earth-turn wording. The scored check does not need that wording.

## liverpool-manchester-1830

The Liverpool and Manchester Railway opened in 1830. It was the first railway in England to connect two major cities.
[Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Liverpool-and-Manchester-Railway). HIGH.

## rocket-trial-36-mph

In the 1829 trials the Rocket, built by George Stephenson and his son Robert, won with a speed of 36 miles (58 km) per hour.
[Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Liverpool-and-Manchester-Railway). HIGH.
This is the trial speed, not a timetable for opening day.

## track-length-conflict

Britannica calls the line a 30-mile line (same page). The Gazette calls the building of it a 35-mile line.
[The Gazette](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/content/100741). Both seen 2026-11-10.
Not taught. No check uses a length.

## cable-1866-hearts-content

On 13 July 1866 the Great Eastern left Valentia, Ireland. On 27 July 1866 the cable landed at Heart's Content, Newfoundland. It was a permanent electrical link.
[IEEE milestone](https://ethw.org/Milestones:Landing_of_the_Transatlantic_Cable,_1866). HIGH.
The milestone's phrase "old world and the new" is not used.

## cable-1852-miles-14-days

The same IEEE page: fourteen days later, 1,852 miles of that cable lay on the ocean floor.
HIGH for those two figures. Not converted to nautical miles.
14 × 132 = 1,848, so 1,852 − 1,848 = 4, and 1,852 ÷ 14 is a little over 132.
The 10-18 check asks which of 100, 130, or 200 is closest. Answer: 130.

[HistoricPlaces.ca](https://www.historicplaces.ca/en/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=3057) confirms the station and gives no coordinates.

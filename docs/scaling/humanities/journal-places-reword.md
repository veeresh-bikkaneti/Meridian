# Journal places, reworded to talk only about the place

**Status:** PROPOSED, 2026-11-10. GATED: no named human educator has signed
`educator-gate-rubric.md`. Owner decision 5 (2026-11-10, logged in
`humanities-definitions-spec.md`, "Owner decisions (2026-11-10)"): keep the
live journal places, and reword Angkor, Potala, Timbuktu and Uluru so they
talk only about the place: architecture, size, age, materials, geography,
height, distance, and the math you can do with them. No religion, belief,
ritual, sacred status or conflict is named.

**This file is the wording only.** The live text sits in files this branch
may not edit. The integrator applies it (see "Files the integrator must
change" below). Nothing here changes the live game.

## Rules applied to every line

- Only place facts: what it is made of, how big, how high, how old, where.
- No words for religion, belief, ritual, worship, pilgrims, sacred status,
  holy places, rulers' religious roles, or any war, attack or dispute.
  Building types that name a faith (temple, mosque, monastery, shrine) are
  replaced by plain words (stone complex, mud-brick buildings, palace).
- Every number has a row in the source table, or it is marked UNVERIFIED and
  is not used in a line a child hears or reads.
- Years-ago answers are worked out at play time as `currentYear − year`;
  no file stores today's year.
- Opening line stays the owner's: "Hey kids, have you heard about this place?"

## 1. Angkor Wat (`angkor`)

**Why it changes.** The live story says it was "built … as a Hindu monument
and later became a Buddhist one". The live narration says "the temple", and
the image alt says "the great temple".

| Field | Proposed text |
|---|---|
| story | "This huge stone complex near Siem Reap in Cambodia was built in the early 12th century. It covers about 160 hectares, and its five towers are the aim point." |
| imageAlt | "Storybook drawing of Angkor Wat, the great stone complex with its five towers reflected in the pond at sunrise." |
| narration (after the opener) | "Five stone towers over a wide moat. Look! The sun lights the stones! Shimmering in the water." |

**Math it supports (PROPOSED, for the educator to place):**

| Group | Question | Answer | Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7-10 | "The bridge to Angkor Wat is 188 metres long. You walk across and back. How far is that?" | 376 m | A3 |
| 7-10 | "Angkor Wat has 5 towers. How many towers would 3 Angkor Wats have?" | 15 | A4 |
| 10-18 | "The Angkor park covers about 400 square kilometres. Angkor Wat covers about 1.6 square kilometres (160 hectares). About how many Angkor Wats would fit in the park?" | 250 | A1, A2 |
| 18+ | "Building took about three decades and began in a reign that started in 1113. About how many years ago did building start?" | about `currentYear − 1113` (an upper bound: the start year is not known exactly) | A5, A6 |

## 2. Potala Palace (`potala`)

**Why it changes.** The live story says the hill "was already sacred" and
names the palace as a religious leader's winter home. The live narration
says "a sacred hill" and "pilgrims climbing up", and the image alt shows
"pilgrims below".

| Field | Proposed text |
|---|---|
| story | "The palace sits on Red Mountain in the Lhasa Valley, about 3,700 metres above sea level. Its thick walls of rammed earth and stone hold more than 1,000 rooms." |
| imageAlt | "Storybook drawing of the Potala Palace, the great white and red palace rising on its rocky hill above Lhasa." |
| narration (after the opener) | "A palace rises on a rocky hill. Look, white walls and red walls! Flags flutter in the wind." |

**Math it supports (PROPOSED):**

| Group | Question | Answer | Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7-10 | "The White Palace was finished in 1648 and the Red Palace in 1694. How many years apart is that?" | 46 | P3, P4 |
| 10-18 | "The White Palace was finished in 1648. How many years ago was that?" | `currentYear − 1648` | P3 |
| 18+ | "The palace has more than 1,000 rooms. If you spent 15 minutes in each room, at least how many hours would you need?" | at least 250 hours (1,000 × 15 = 15,000 minutes) | P5 |

**Height above the valley is UNVERIFIED (sources disagree):** Britannica
gives 130 m (425 ft) above the Lhasa River valley; the Japanese summary on
the UNESCO page gives 110 m. Neither number is used until one source is
chosen (P6).

**Extra screen note (for the owner):** this place's story is closely tied to
a living political dispute over the region. The wording above names only the
city, the valley and the mountain, and names no country, government or ruler.
If the owner judges the place itself too close to contested politics, the
§7a rule is "when in doubt, leave it out": the integrator would drop the
entry rather than soften it further. Logged, not decided here.

## 3. Timbuktu (`timbuktu`)

**Why it changes.** The live story names a religion's learning and its
buildings by faith. The live narration says "the mosques stand over it all",
and the image alt names "mosques".

| Field | Proposed text |
|---|---|
| story | "The city in Mali stands at the edge of the Sahara Desert. It was a trading crossroads where salt, gold and books were bought and sold, and its great mud-brick buildings still mark the old center." |
| imageAlt | "Storybook drawing of Timbuktu, the ancient mud-brick buildings and a library with a camel caravan in the desert." |
| narration (after the opener) | "Camels carry goods across the desert! And the library keeps old stories safe! Mud-brick walls stand over it all." |

**Math it supports (PROPOSED):**

| Group | Question | Answer | Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7-10 | "Builders rebuilt one great building from 1570 to 1583. How many years did that take?" | 13 | T4 |
| 10-18 | "In the 1500s about 100,000 people lived in Timbuktu. If 4 people share one house, how many houses is that?" | 25,000 (fictional household size, labelled as such) | T3 |
| 18+ | "Timbuktu was founded in the 5th century. At least how many years ago is that?" | at least `currentYear − 500` | T1 |

The river near the city is not named in the UNESCO text used here; its name
stays out until sourced (T6, UNVERIFIED).

## 4. Uluru (`uluru`)

**Why it changes.** The live story and narration call it "a sacred place for
the Anangu people" / "Sacred to the Anangu people".

| Field | Proposed text |
|---|---|
| story | "The huge red sandstone rock rises from the desert in Australia's Northern Territory. It stands 348 metres high and is 9.4 kilometres around." |
| imageAlt | unchanged: "Storybook drawing of Uluru, the great red sandstone monolith glowing at sunset with visitors viewing from afar." |
| narration (after the opener) | "A giant red rock in the desert. Look! The rock glows in the sunset! Quiet and gold." |

**Math it supports (PROPOSED):**

| Group | Question | Answer | Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7-10 | "Uluru is 9.4 km around. You walk around it twice. How far is that?" | 18.8 km | U2 |
| 10-18 | "Uluru is 348 m high. A tower is 116 m high. How many towers stacked up make Uluru?" | 3 (the tower height is fictional, labelled as such) | U1 |
| 18+ | "If Uluru were a perfect circle 9.4 km around, about how wide would it be? (C = πd)" | about 3.0 km (9.4 ÷ 3.14 ≈ 2.99) | U2; the circle is a model, not a fact about the rock |
